Back in September 2024, I posted a list of hiking ideas, including
[Nebelhorn (2224 m)](/404/) and [Simetsberg (1840 m)](/404/). This ridge got on
the list and remained there until Mikuláš finally convinced me to do it. With
no counterarguments, I agreed and asked Maksym, a former DAAD scholar, to join.

![Sillbach waterfall](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-00.jpg)

We started off at the frozen Sillbach waterfall near Bayrischzell. Immediately,
I realized my mistake of not bringing crampons. The asphalt was icy, and I
slipped all over the place. There was a very small section of the trail without
snow, but it was too short to make a difference. Soon, we were going through
a snowy trail in the forest.

![Snowy trail along the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-01.jpg)

As we reached Fellalm-Sattel (1645 m), the snowstorm intensified. The wind was
howling, and visibility dropped significantly. We debated whether to continue or
turn back. It was really disappointing to only visit the first peak or to turn
back without reaching any summit. However, we decided to press on, hoping for
better conditions at higher altitudes.

![View from Fellalm-Sattel (1645 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-02.jpg)

The path from the saddle to the slope of Großer Traithen (1852 m) was the worst
part of the hike. The wind was relentless, and the snow was blowing
horizontally, making it difficult to see. It also got freezing real fast. When
the slope started, the route got much better. The wind was blocked by the
mountain and the pine trees. We could finally enjoy the hike without battling
the elements.

![View from the slope onto Fellalm-Sattel (1645 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-03.jpg)

When we reached the summit of Großer Traithen (1852 m), the wind picked up
again. Still, we found a spot between the rocks to have a quick snack and take
in the leftovers of the view. Although we were happy to have made it to the
top, we realized that the ridge was just starting, and we had a long way to go
to reach Oberaudorf.

![View from Großer Traithen (1852 m) towards Unterbergerjoch (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-04.jpg)
![Our group on the top of Großer Traithen (1852 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-05.jpg)

The way to Unterbergerjoch (1796 m) was relatively easy, with a gentle descent.
The wind settled down, and we could enjoy the scenery. At that point, we still
haven't seen any other hikers, which was surprising given the popularity of the
area.

![View from Unterbergerjoch (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-06.jpg)
![Karl Ruppert memorial plaque](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-07.jpg)

We kept going towards the next peak, and eventually passed by the Karl Ruppert
memorial plaque. Somehow, it wasn't covered by snow despite the harsh weather.
As we continued towards Steilner Joch (1747 m), we actually saw a hiker
approaching from the valley. At that point, we were pleasantly surprised to see
other people on the trail. Now, however, I'm even more surprised. The path
from Seeon Alm to the peak has a level A via ferrata section, but this didn't
seem to deter the hiker.

![Going towards Steilner Joch (1747 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-08.jpg)
![The hiker going back to Unterbergerjoch (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-09.jpg)

The Steilner Joch (1747 m) summit itself was uneventful. The view from it is
roughly the same as the previous peaks. However, the lower elevation meant that
the scenery was hidden by the other mountains. This mountaintop is the last one
on the more or less level ridge before the steep descent to Oberaudorf. Once we
saw the descent, we were eager to complete the challenge and head down to the
town.

![Mikuláš Netík at Steilner Joch (1747 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-10.jpg)
![The Steilner Grat ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-11.jpg)

The last part of Steilner Grat was a bit annoying. The top of the ridge was
covered with a rock wall and a spiky fence, forcing us to go around it and
cross over it multiple times. I'm pretty sure I damaged my pants on the rocks.
We also passed [Brünnstein (1634 m)](/hikes/2025-07-06-bruennstein/), which I
decided to climb later in the year from the other side.

![The sign at Steilner Joch (1747 m) pointing back up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-12.jpg)
![View at Brünnstein (1634 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-13.jpg)

The rest of the way was quite straightforward. We descended through the forest,
occasionally stopping to admire the view and look back at the ridge we had just
crossed. Some sections of the road were clear of snow, making the hike easier.
On the other hand, some parts were still covered in ice, requiring careful
footing.

![View back at Steilner Grat](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-14.jpg)
![Descend towards Oberaudorf](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-15.jpg)

We didn't have time to be careful though, as we needed to catch the train back
to Munich. We hurried down the trail, passing the helicopter landing site in
Rechenau and some snow clearing machines. Finally, we reached Oberaudorf and
made it to the train station just in time.

![Snow clearing machine in Rechenau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_traithen/2025-01-26-16.jpg)

Overall, the hike along Steilner Grat was a challenging but rewarding
experience. Despite the harsh weather conditions, we managed to complete the
entire ridge and enjoy the stunning views along the way. I would definitely
recommend this hike to others, but in winter, make sure to bring proper gear
like crampons and gaiters, and be prepared for changing weather conditions.
