Topo from [stadler-markus.de](https://www.stadler-markus.de/klettern/breitenstein-und-schwarzwand.html):
![Topo of the climb](/images/hikes/topo/breitensteinfensterl.png)

After doing the [Breitenstein (1622 m)](/hikes/2025-05-18-breitenstein_wendelstein/)
hike with David a while ago, I was fascinated by the rock formation called
Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m), seeing photos of it online.

![Ruins I mentioned in the Breitenstein (1622 m) post](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-00.jpg)
![Forest on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-01.jpg)

I started the hike the same way as for my trip to Breitenstein (1622 m), going
up the forest road while passing other hikers. The weather was quite hot, and
some of the passerby hikers took off their shirts to cool down. There were tons
of bugs around, with butterflies fluttering everywhere.

![A butterfly on my shorts](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-02.jpg)
![Lady's smock aka cuckoo flower aka cardamine pratensis](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-03.jpg)

At some point, I reached a fork in the road. While most hikers followed the
wide path up to the main peak, I turned into high grass and bushes, following a
smaller trail that led to the Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m). After I passed
a few hikers with a dog right near the rock formation, I started to prepare for
the climb.

![Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-08.jpg)
![Me at the top of Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-04.jpg)

The climb starts with a short section without any protection. You have to grab
the rock and pull yourself up. After that, the cable starts, and you can clip
in your via ferrata set. The climb is quite exposed, with wind constantly
blowing on you. Next comes the Drahtseil, the narrow section with drops on both
sides. Afterwards, it's a straightforward climb to the top.

![The view from Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-05.jpg)
![Me drinking my last Prime (still disgusting)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-06.jpg)

After collecting myself at the top, I headed back down the same way I came up.
The descent was even scarier, having to backtrack the exposed sections. But I
managed to make it down safely. As I reached the base, another pair of older
hikers came up to pass by the rock formation. I decided to have some snacks and
take in the view while talking to them.

![Another butterfly on my shirt](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-07.jpg)
![Mountain cornflower aka bachelor's button](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-09.jpg)

Although I came to climb the Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m), I still had a lot
of energy left. So, I continued up to the main Breitenstein (1622 m) peak,
following a small trail between trees and rocks. It seemed like a popular spot
for rock climbers, as many of the rocks had markings on them.

![Rocks on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-10.jpg)
![The view on the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-11.jpg)

Eventually, I reached the bare hillside of the mountain, with a clear view of
the surroundings. The whole slope was covered in berries, which I happily
picked up and ate. Thankfully, I had brought enough water to wash them down.
Soon, I saw the summit cross of Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m), with some
hikers on the surrounding rocks.

![The Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-12.jpg)
![The cross at Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-15.jpg)

I ate my last snack at the summit, enjoying the panoramic view of the
mountains. I started feeling thirsty again, but I had no water left after
eating the berries. I had several choices: ask other hikers for water, go back
down to Birkenstein in search of a water source, or continue to the main
Breitenstein (1622 m) peak and hope to find water in the nearby hut.

![View down from Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-13.jpg)
![View from Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m) towards Breitenstein (1622 m) and Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-14.jpg)

The closest hut was near the main summit, so I decided to continue there. The
trail leading there was quite interesting, with a small section going through
the bushes. I had to jump over a small hole between the rocks, which made the
route more fun. Finally, I reached the summit of Breitenstein (1622 m),
where I stayed just for a moment to take in the view. It was nice seeing
[Wendelstein (1838 m)](/404/) in the distance again.

![A puddle on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-16.jpg)
![View down from Breitenstein (1622 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-17.jpg)

I headed down to Hubertushütte, hoping to get a drink there. However, the hut
was already closed for the day, so I had to proceed. I knew my last option for
getting water was at the Kesselalm (1278 m), so I hurried down the trail to
get there before it closed.

![View from Breitenstein (1622 m) towards Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-18.jpg)
![Road down to Kesselalm (1278 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-19.jpg)

When I reached the hut, there were only two people sitting outside. I quickly
entered and asked if they were still open. The lady nodded and told me to
order quickly, as they were about to close. I ordered some Skiwasser and a
glass of Almdudler, which I drank eagerly. The hiker toilets were also closed
for the day, but they kindly let me use the ones inside the hut.

![The life-saving drinks on display](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-20.jpg)

After refreshing myself, I started the final descent back to Birkenstein. I
took the same trail as on my [Easter hike to Schweinsberg (1514 m)](/hikes/2025-04-21-schweinsberg/),
so I was familiar with the route. The walk was peaceful, with the sun
setting behind the hills. I reached Birkenstein just a few minutes before the
bus drove by, so I took it back to the train station.

![The Easter egg hunting spot from the Schweinsberg (1514 m) hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/breitensteinfensterl/2025-07-20-21.jpg)

All in all, it was a great hike with an unforgettable climb. I would only
recommend the climb to experienced hikers with a good head for heights, as it
is not for the faint of heart. But if you are up for the challenge, the
Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m) is definitely worth the effort! And the rest of
the hike is accessible to all hikers, with beautiful views and interesting
trails. You also have an option to turn west from Breitenstein Westgipfel
(1575 m) and go down to the famous [Café Winklstüberl](https://www.winklstueberl.de/).
