This adventure started off slow and sad. The whole sky was covered in clouds
and the weather forecast promised rain and snow for the entire day, even a
snowstorm in some places. When I and Bohdan boarded the bus to the ravine,
there were 4 passengers in total, all getting off at the same stop.

![Schwarzenbach river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-00.jpg)
![Totems on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-01.jpg)

We wished good luck upon the other hikers and started our journey. A big part
of this route goes along a forest road, making it fairly easy to traverse in
any season. We even met a guy walking a fluffy dog in the opposite direction.

![Approaching a meadow](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-02.jpg)

I desperately tried to see any of the surrounding mountains, but the visibility
was so bad I could only see 20-30 meters in front of me. Could be worse, I
guess.

![Schwarzentennalm from up close](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-03.jpg)
![Me walking up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-05.jpg)

Around the midway we came across [Schwarzentennalm](https://www.schwarzentennalm.de/),
the local huts with some people inside. Probably they were the reason for the
road being cleared of snow. Unfortunately, soon after the huts we made a sharp
turn and from that point onwards we had to drag our feet through the fresh
fluffy snow at the end of March.

![Approaching the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-06.jpg)
![Gurnbach river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-07.jpg)

At first, the level of snow was reasonable, and we could see Skitour tracks in
the snow. We assumed they led to a nearby peak or at least a hill on the way,
so we chose to follow them as much as we could.

![Crossing the Gurnbach river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-08.jpg)
![Bohdan entering the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-09.jpg)

We saw a lot of cool things: some bridges over mountain streams, cattle fences,
and more huts in the distance. As the elevation grew and the path became
steeper, the snow grew deeper, and it got harder to traverse. The whole time I
was walking behind Bohdan, so at least I didn't have to be the first one
digging the snow with every step.

![A hut covered in snow on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-10.jpg)
![Looking at Schwarzeck (1382 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-11.jpg)

Eventually, we crossed a ridge and got to higher elevation huts, Rauhalmhütte,
near the bottom of Seekarkreuz (1601 m), that I visited [plenty of times before](/hikes/2025-12-14-lainbachfall_jochberg/).

![Climbing up to Rauhalmhütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-12.jpg)
![Crossing a ridge to Rauhalmhütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-13.jpg)

Since the huts are in a basin surrounded by a ridge, the visibility was
enough to see the other side of it, so I can even claim I saw some nice views.

![Bohdan approaching Rauhalmhütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-14.jpg)
![Hiking up to the saddle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-15.jpg)

We deviated a bit from the planned route and took a shortcut directly up the
mountain, skipping the huts. I didn't look too close at the map beforehand, so
I didn't even realize we were a few dozen meters away from
Seekarkreuz (1601 m). When we looked back at the huts, we saw a person
following our footsteps. In retrospect, they were probably going to the nearest
peak and then down to Lenggries, while we planned a much more challenging route.

![A sign on the saddle towards Seekarkreuz (1601 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-16.jpg)
![A tree on the way to Brandkopf (1569 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-17.jpg)

We walked onto the ridge going to Brandkopf (1569 m) and started the real climb.
Thankfully, the elevation gain was not that big, so we could ascend reasonably
fast. The snow was getting deeper, but with Bohdan in the front, it was still
manageable. We walked past a curious dry tree, which looked like it came from a
fantasy novel, and continued to the top.

![Frozen tree on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-18.jpg)
![View from Brandkopf (1569 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-19.jpg)

Once we reached Brandkopf (1569 m), we took a quick break for some photos, then
continued onwards. The snowstorm calmed down, now being replaced with a mist
and some light snowfall. With the situation at hand, we didn't stay to see the
potential views, instead opted to go forward.

![Descending from Brandkopf (1569 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-20.jpg)
![Me going down the steep slope of Brandkopf (1569 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-21.jpg)

We had no idea where the trail down to the saddle was, so Bohdan was just
experimenting with the descent, and I was following his path. As we reached the
saddle, we started going up, and passed a warning sign saying the next section
is for experienced hikers only.

![Crossing the saddle from Brandkopf (1569 m) to Spitzkamp (1604 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-22.jpg)
![Frozen nature on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-23.jpg)

I've slightly underestimated the amount of experience one needs. The ascent
quickly turned into a climb up the rocks. While it's probably fine in dry
summer weather, in winter with a deep level of snow it's a different story.
At any point you can miss an edge of a rock, step on some loose ground, or slip
on one of the metal poles invisible under the layers upon layers of snowfall.
Either mishap leads to you slipping down, and the route is so steep that
slipping should not be seen as an accident you can walk away from without any
damage.

![The beginning of the challenging climb to Spitzkamp (1604 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-24.jpg)
![Me climbing up to Spitzkamp (1604 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-43.jpg)

Bohdan was much more confident and moved forward with grace, while I had a
different experience. After my [fall from Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/)
and the subsequent [retrieval of a lost hiking stick](/hikes/2026-03-22-unterammergau_sonnenberg/),
I have been a bit more cautious on the trails, and this one was no exception.
I was trying to be careful with every step, but the snow was so deep that I
couldn't see any of the rocks or poles, and I had to trust my feet to find the
right path. It was a nerve-wracking experience, and I was constantly on edge,
afraid of slipping and falling down the mountain.

![Looking at the Spitzkamp (1604 m) cross from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-25.jpg)

Bohdan was very understanding, waiting for me while laughing and filming me
from above, all in good fun. Eventually I overcame my fear and climbed to the
top, where the Spitzkamp (1604 m) peak was waiting with proverbial open arms.

![The anxiety-inducing ridge of Spitzkamp (1604 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-26.jpg)

We stayed for a bit to look around and panic took over me again. There was
nowhere to go! South-west was the way we came from, and I had the feeling I
wouldn't be able to climb down the path I took. North-west and south-east was
the drop-off of the cliff. That obviously wasn't an option either. The only
choice was to go forward, but the path looked very narrow and sketchy, with
most of the snow around barely staying on top of buried bushes and trees.

![Me taking on the ridge of Spitzkamp (1604 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-27.jpg)
![An easier section of the Kampen ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-28.jpg)

I was quite nervous walking on the thin cliffside covered in knee-deep snow,
but thankfully this section finally ended after around 200 meters. What
followed was a path on the safer side, although still covered in deep snow.
Hiking in winter is in general a different challenge from the usual tracks.
Wherever you go, you have to lift the legs high up to get out of snow, usually
having to push the legs up with force to get them out of more solid layers.
Thus, you use not only the usual walking/hiking muscles, but also some others
that might not be as well-trained.

![The Auerkamp (1607 m) hill from afar](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-29.jpg)

As we continued, we walked past a small hill on the cliffside covered in
bushes. It was clear that was supposed to be Auerkamp (1607 m), but there was
no cross or register to be found, and it wasn't a smart idea to fight the
bushes to climb there, so we kept going.

![Memorial plaque near Auerkamp (1607 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-30.jpg)
![Continuing the route from Auerkamp (1607 m) to Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-31.jpg)

There was an extra cross further ahead at the memorial plaque, which I took
a photo at to count as the previous peak. The way at this point got quite
wide, and we had multiple options when walking around trees and piles of snow.

![Approaching Bohdan at Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-32.jpg)
![View down from Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-33.jpg)

We kept going for a bit more and got to the final peak of the ridge, Ochsenkamp
(1594 m). Although we were happy to complete the climbing part of the hike, we
were a bit confused with the road leading to it, since we didn't see a trail
branching off from the route we took to go down the mountain.

![Starting the descent from Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-34.jpg)

Regardless, we stayed there a bit to take photos, I took a break to catch my
breath and enjoy the would-be views of Hirschberg (1670 m), another great
medium-difficulty peak I [visited in the past](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/).

![A sharp turn on the way down from Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-35.jpg)

We found the rough location of the trail, took some wrong turns, and ended up
on a steep slope going straight down the mountain. Since it became clear I
gave us wrong directions (trying out the map feature of Garmin Fenix 8), Bohdan
went straight across the trees and bushes onto the correct trail, and I spent
several minutes trying to cross over to him too.

![The Heinz Ebenbeck memorial near Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-36.jpg)

The descent was quite steep all the way until the Heinz Ebenbeck memorial,
situated on a big rock on the way down. The man died in an avalanche in 1987,
and this plaque served us as a good reminder to be careful on our trips.

![Reaching the mixed forest treeline](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-37.jpg)

After the memorial, we quickly reached the treeline of a mixed forest, and I
got another exciting hillside walk down through the forest itself. It was a
nice change of pace to be able to comfortably descend without being careful
with every step like higher up in the mountains.

![Passing Hirschtalsattel (1227 m) with a view onto Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-38.jpg)

At last, Bohdan reached Hirschtalsattel (1227 m). I, on the other hand, was
way behind, to the point he thought I got lost. Thankfully, I emerged from
the forest soon after, and we started going down a wide gravel road.

![River Hirschbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-39.jpg)

To our detriment, the road was covered in deep snow too, but there was a pair
of footsteps of a person trying to climb up one of the surrounding mountains
and giving up immediately. We used the holes in the snow that the person made,
which sped up our travel back by a lot.

![Approaching Lenggries farms](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-40.jpg)

As we kept getting closer to Lenggries, the snow was also getting more shallow,
so we managed to maintain a decent pace and aim for an earlier train. As we
were walking through the forest, I kept getting a weird feeling that I've been
there before. I checked the map and saw that we were approaching the trail that
[branches off to Seekarkreuz (1601 m)](/hikes/2025-03-02-seekarkreuz/).

![Horses near Lenggries](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-41.jpg)

We were still some distance away from it, making me even more confused. Turns
out, I took the same road on my hike to [Fockenstein (1564 m)](/404/) with
Maksym. Although I haven't been on this trail for years, the memories of it
hidden deep within started coming to the surface. I didn't have much time to
dwell on it though, as we had to hurry towards the train station.

![A tree bloom in Lenggries](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/lenggries_auerkamp/2026-03-29-42.jpg)

A few waterfalls and huts later, we finally got out of the forest and onto the
fields and meadows of Lenggries. [Schloss Hohenburg](http://schloss.st-ursula.net/)
came and went, and we were already in the town. I made a bet with Bohdan on how
many hikers we'll come across on the hike, saying 12 (14 including the ones
taking the bus). It fell through, since we only saw 4. Anyway, we quickly
reached the station and went home.

All in all, I am sure this hike offers nice views and challenging paths at a
better time of the year, but right now it wasn't that exciting. Zero
visibility, freezing wind, knee-deep snow, and steep cliffs make this hike a
bad idea at this time of the year.
