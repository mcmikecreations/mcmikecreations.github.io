The hike started off with me and Maksym catching up after not seeing each other
for quite some time. Although the route I planned was quite long, he was happy
to join, and I trusted his fitness. The way near Lenggries was nice, the
sunscreen helped against the scorching sun, and the route between the trees was
bearable.

![Maksym starting the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-00.jpg)

The next section of the hike was improvised. The trail we were planning to take
was barely marked on the map, and it showed. Overgrown with grass and ferns, it
was hidden from sight, so we took the straight way up to the ridge near
Bergstation Schrödelsteinbahn. There's a cliff some people beside us were
climbing, which was really cool to see from the side.

![View onto Bergstation Schrödelsteinbahn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-01.jpg)

Once we got to the top of the ridge, we saw quite a view. Behind us was
Brauneck (1555 m), with paragliders flying up from it. On the other side were
all the hills and peaks towards Benediktenwand (1801 m). Towards the south,
we were greeted with Jachenau and all of the surrounding mountains there.

![View from Stangeneck (1646 m) towards Brauneck (1555 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-02.jpg)

When we reached Stangeneck (1646 m), we didn't find much to look at. No fancy
cross, no groups of tourists resting there. It was just a transit point towards
bigger summits ahead. Still, it was nice to look around and take a short break.

![Panorama near Vorderer-Kirchstein (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-03.jpg)

When we were walking near Vorderer-Kirchstein (1670 m), the peak there was
slightly elevated near the route. In retrospect, we should have visited it, but
being already tired and having many kilometers ahead, we skipped it altogether.

![View from Latschenkopf (1712 m) onto Stiealm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-04.jpg)
![View from Latschenkopf (1712 m) onto Vorderer-Kirchstein (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-05.jpg)

Next up was Latschenkopf (1712 m), one of the cross-abled summits on our way.
It was a bit fancier than some others, with the Stiealm mountain hut underneath
and forests surrounding it. There's also a detour to
Hinterer Kirchstein (1667 m), but we also skipped it.

![View from Latschenkopf (1712 m) onto Latschenkopf (1488 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-06.jpg)

After crossing Probstalmsattel (1628 m), the last possible detour we could take
on the way, we decided to commit, visit Achselköpfe (1710 m), and proceed onto
Benediktenwand (1801 m). The Achselköpfe (1710 m) was wilder than we expected.
With drop-offs on both sides, the narrow trail surrounded by bushes and spruce
vegetation led us straight to a Benediktenwand (1801 m) view.

![View from Achselköpfe (1710 m) onto Benediktenwand (1801 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-07.jpg)

The last bit of the way to the final peak also caught us off-guard. There's a
very small grade-A via ferrata section there, which Maksym hasn't experienced
before. He was tired and hungry, hot from the sun and dry from the depleted
water supply we had. Once we reached the top, we took some photos, and started
checking them out. In the background of the photos, we saw some really dark
clouds, and looking up confirmed our worries.

![View from Benediktenwand (1801 m) towards Walchensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-08.jpg)
![An ibex goat on Benediktenwand (1801 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-09.jpg)

We quickly packed our stuff and headed downhill, with the rainy clouds turning
into a full-blown thunderstorm. Although the way towards Tutzinger Hütte was
quite steep with some smooth stones that turned wet and slippery, we had no
choice and rushed down. On the upside, we saw an ibex goat hiding from the rain
in an opening on the mountain, which was nice. Apparently, that's the mascot of
the mountain ridge, plastered all over merchendise and postcards.

![Descent from Benediktenwand (1801 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-10.jpg)
![Rainy Benediktenwand (1801 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-11.jpg)

Tutzinger Hütte themselves were nice too. We got some warm drinks and stayed a
bit to dry up, hoping the rain would be over soon. Our phones were useless and
caught no internet, so we tried asking around for weather reports. One of the
hikers asking for an overnight stay told us the rain will continue all the way
into the evening, so there was no point leaving the hut. Well, we had other
plans - a working day on Monday morning.

We waited for the worst part of the thunderstorm to subside and headed outside.
Trying to hide under the trees, we kept going through the slippery trails,
falling into deep rain puddles and grabbing onto surrounding bushes for
balance. The local black salamanders took center stage here, forcing us to
avoid them among other obstacles while running downhill. Although we saw more
than 50 of them, we didn't take any photos, since the phones were also soaked.

![Views between Benediktenwand (1801 m) and Kochel](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/benediktenwand/2024-07-21-12.jpg)

Once we entered a wider dirt road towards Kochel, we took a wrong turn onto
a hunter's trail going nowhere, and waster an hour walking there and back.
The rain was still tough, but at that point we got used to it. After going
back onto the correct trail we finally exited the mountainside and entered
Pessenbach, a local settlement near Kochelsee. Although the worst was over
and even the rain slowed down, we still had to walk all the way to Kochel,
with not a single car stopping to pick our wet butts up.

To recap, this route offers some amazing views of the ridge and everything
around it, and there are plenty of peaks you can visit by making small
detours. The hike is definitely on the hard side, but there's nothing
particularly dangerous there, just a lot of walking. Also, for more
casual hikers there's plenty of crossings where you can end the hike early and
head home. Try it out!
