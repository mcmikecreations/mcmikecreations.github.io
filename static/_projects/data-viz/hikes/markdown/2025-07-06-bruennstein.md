![Topo of the climb](/images/hikes/topo/bruennstein.png)

I don't remember what came first: our plan to climb Zugspitze (2962 m) over
Höllental which [we later did](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/), or
the idea to start rock climbing indoors. Either way, we bought a climbing
harness and a via ferrata set, so we wanted to try them out on a real climb.

![A butterfly sitting on David's backpack](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-00.jpg)

After scrolling through several climbing websites and looking at the available
options, we settled on Brünnstein (1619 m), a mountain in the Bavarian Alps
known for a nice beginner-friendly via ferrata route.

![The view at the start of the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-01.jpg)
![Tatzelwurmwasserfälle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-23.jpg)

We looked up the closest public transport options and found that we could take
the bus crossing the mountains between Bayrischzell and Flintsbach and get off
at the Tatzelwurmwasserfälle. The waterfalls were amazing, which set the tone
for the rest of the hike.

![The nettle-leaved bellflower aka campanula trachelium](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-02.jpg)

Despite passing many hiking groups at the beginning of the trail, we barely
saw anyone going on our route. It would be interesting to know what other
exciting hikes are there that are more popular than this one. Maybe
Steilnerjoch (1747 m), which I crossed on my
[Großer Traithen (1852 m) hike](/hikes/2025-01-26-grosser_traithen/)?

![Brünnstein (1619 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-03.jpg)

We kept going and came across a big meadow full of cows. That's where David
said "Oh, Wendelstein!" (1838 m) after recognizing the peak we
[visited in the past](/hikes/2025-05-18-breitenstein_wendelstein/). For some
reason, I remembered that phrase ever since. It feels nice to see the hiking
progress of a friend reflected in their growing mountain recognition skills.
I remember our first hike together, [Rampoldplatte (1422 m)](/hikes/2025-02-09-rampoldplatte/),
when David experienced a medium-difficulty snowy peak as his first climb.

![Cows on the meadow](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-04.jpg)
![A clear meadow with a nice view](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-05.jpg)

Closer to Brünnsteinhaus (1342 m), there was a tiny section with some rocks
and a cable. We thought the via ferrata had already started, but it was a false
call. After reaching the hut, we saw dark clouds on the horizon, so we quickly
put on the gear, started the climb, and hoped for the best.

![The view from Brünnsteinhaus (1342 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-06.jpg)

The climb was great! It was easy overall, but had a tiny tunnel section, some
stairs and rocks to climb, and a few exposed parts with nice views. The weather
held up, and we reached the summit without any rain. You can see the full climb
in 360° below. It works on any device, but on a phone with a gyroscope, you can
move the phone around to look in different directions. Make sure to watch it in
high resolution (click the gear icon) for the best experience. Subtitles are
included!

![The 360° climb of Brünnstein (1619 m)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5yRsnelCsw)

On the summit, we found the famous "Dr. Julius Mayr Weg" sign that everyone
takes photos with. You can find many instances of it on the Internet, but here
is my own contribution. The view from the top was beautiful, with many peaks
visible in the distance. At that time, I hadn't explored many of them, so it
was exciting to see them from afar and plan future hikes.

![The "Dr. Julius Mayr Weg" sign](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-07.jpg)
![The view from the sign](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-08.jpg)

Looking behind towards Austria, we saw a bunch of higher mountains. I don't
think I paid much attension during the hike, but those were the Kaisergebirge
and Pyramidenspitze (1997 m), which I [climbed in winter](/hikes/2025-02-02-pyramidenspitze/).

![View towards Pyramidenspitze (1997 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-09.jpg)
![Looking at Brünnsteinkapelle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-10.jpg)

As we were filling out the summit logbook, dark clouds started rolling in
quickly. We decided to descend as fast as possible, taking the normal hiking
trail down. Just as we reached Brünnsteinhaus (1342 m), the rain started
pouring heavily. We took shelter in the hut, had some snacks and drinks,
and waited for the storm to pass.

![View from the summit towards Trainsjoch (1707 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-11.jpg)
![David climbing down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-12.jpg)

As the rain wasn't stopping, we decided to head out and descend towards
Oberaudorf. The trail was already slippery, but nothing too bad. We were happy
to have completed the climb and enjoyed the adventure despite the weather.
If the rain had started earlier, we would have had to complete the climb in the
downpour, which would have been much more challenging and less enjoyable.

![Cloudy view from Brünnsteinhaus (1342 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-13.jpg)
![View westwards from Brünnsteinhaus (1342 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-14.jpg)

After going through the forest, we came out into a hillside meadow with a ski
slope. It was still raining lightly, but the sun was slowly coming out. The
dirt road had ended and turned into a paved road, which made the walk easier.

![Wild Bavarian Valeriana on the way back](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-15.jpg)
![Martagon lily aka Turk's cap lily in the forest](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-16.jpg)

I've been on this slope before, as it was my first experience with skiing in
Germany back in February. It was fun to see the place in summer, with green
grass and flowers instead of snow. I remember struggling to stay upright on
the skis, but at least I enjoyed it!

![A hunter's hut on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-17.jpg)
![Exiting the forest](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-18.jpg)

Back then, the asphalt road and the surrounding area was completely bare of
snow, so it was dangerous to ski down there. This time, it was a pleasant walk
down the hill, with nice views of the valley and the mountains in the distance.

![The top station of the ski resort](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-19.jpg)
![The cable car guarding the ski lift](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-20.jpg)

At some point, the sky cleared up enough to reveal a beautiful rainbow over
the valley. It was a perfect end to our hike, seeing local cows and sheep
grazing peacefully under the rainbow. We reached Oberaudorf just in time to
catch the train back to Munich.

![The ski lift going to Oberaudorf](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-21.jpg)
![Local sheep ignoring the rainbow](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bruennstein/2025-07-06-22.jpg)

In general, it was a fantastic day of hiking and climbing, with beautiful
scenery, fun challenges, and a bit of adventure. I would definitely recommend
Brünnstein (1619 m) to anyone looking for a fun and beginner-friendly via
ferrata climb in the area. It's quite safe and secure, so even if you don't
have the gear on you, you can still try it out. I don't recommend going without
gear, of course, but it's up to you. Just be careful and enjoy the climb!
