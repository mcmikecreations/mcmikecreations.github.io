Topo from [bergsteigen.com](https://www.bergsteigen.com/touren/klettersteig/hoellental-klettersteig-zugspitze/):
![Topo of the climb](/images/hikes/topo/zugspitze_hoellental.png)

The idea for this hike was born pretty much immediately after finishing the
[Reintal route to the Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-06-21-zugspitze_reintal/). We
planned ahead, bought an ice axe and ice spikes, checked the weather forecast
and public transport schedule, so nothing would stop us from going up the
Höllental Klamm. I even spend the night before the hike at David's place, so
we could get to the train station faster.

![Trail near Hammersbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-00.jpg)

David watched a lot more videos about the Höllental route than I did, so he
was more aware of the dangers and challenges, hurrying me up all the time. We
took the earliest train from Munich to Garmisch-Partenkirchen, then a bus to
Hammersbach. From there, we started the hike at around 8:30 AM.

![Path to Höllental Klamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-01.jpg)
![Entrance to Höllental Klamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-02.jpg)

The first part of the hike, just like on the Reintal route, is a long
ascent through the forest. This is where we warmed up and got ready for the
more challenging parts of the hike. After some time, we reached the paid
passage of the Höllental Klamm. The entrance fee was quite small, and it was
worth taking this route instead of walking around the gorge. The path itself
is very beautiful, with wooden walkways and bridges built along the cliffs and
over the water. The sound of the rushing water and the narrow passages made
it feel like an adventure. We got quite wet though, as there were many spots
where water splashed onto the walkway from above.

![View onto Hinterer Waxenstein (2268 m) from the gorge exit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-03.jpg)

After exiting the gorge, we continued along a normal hiking trail until we
reached Höllentalangerhütte (1387 m), a mountain hut where we took a break and talked to
other hikers.

![Höllentalangerhütte (1387 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-04.jpg)

We kept going and soon reached the first via ferrata, a ladder with a cable you
can clip into for safety. This part was quite exciting, as it involved some
vertical climbing. It wasn't difficult though, and we managed it without any
issues. I should mention that this is one of the main bottlenecks of the hike,
as it's not really possible to walk around other people to skip the line.

![Höllentalsteig, the first via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-05.jpg)

After some cool vertical and horizontal climbing sections on metal rods
sticking from the rocks, we reached another shallow section between the
climbing parts. David was still worried about the upcoming glacier crossing,
so we hurried up ahead, together with another pair of hikers.

![View behind from the top of Höllentalsteig](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-06.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the glacier. That's when the fact that I didn't even
open the ice spikes box until that moment came back to bite me. David put on
his spikes really fast, while I awkwardly tried to put mine on the uneven
ground, losing the tiny bolts on the rocks and snow. Still, I somehow managed
to put them on and we started crossing the glacier.

![View forward towards the glacier](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-07.jpg)

We asked other hikers about the best route to take, and followed their advice
of going straight up the middle of the glacier. That was a bad mistake, as
that "path" was quite steep and slippery. I struggled a bit, taking my time to
not slip down. It also strained my legs a lot because of the awkward angle of
ascent. David, on the other hand, had much more trouble, as his spikes kept
slipping off his feet.

![View downwards from the glacier](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-08.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the top of the glacier, climbed over a giant crack in
the ice and came up to the main via ferrata section leading to the summit.
If I recall correctly, it took us around 2.5 or even 3 hours to reach the
summit from here, although other hikers promised us it would take only
1.5 hours.

![View up the glacier](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-09.jpg)

At some point, quite early in the climb, I started losing my breath
and needed to take breaks more often. The altitude really affected me this
time. It really reminded me of the [Frau Hitt (2270 m)](/hikes/2025-03-23-frau_hitt/)
hike and the challenges I faced there. David seemed to be doing fine, but
waited for me nonetheless. Still, we took over a few other hikers who were
doing even worse than me.

![The start of Höllental Klettersteig, the main via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-10.jpg)

The main via ferrata section seemed never-ending. We just kept climbing higher
and higher, with rare flat sections to rest. Because the last cable car down
from the summit was at 16:00, we were constantly checking the time and
calculating whether we would make it down in time. This added some stress to
the already challenging climb.

![Seeing the station near the peak, still an hour to go](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-11.jpg)

Eventually, we saw the tourist station at the summit, and David cheered up,
saying we don't have much distance left. However, I already knew from
experience that the remaining elevation to the station was quite large, so I
kept going with the same pace.

![Eibsee from the Irmerscharte saddle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-12.jpg)

After 40-60 more minutes of climbing, we finally saw the cross and the climbing
section was over. The rest of the way to the summit was just an upwards walk,
where I could finally catch my breath again.

![The end of the climb](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-13.jpg)

We reached the summit at around 15:30, just in time to take some photos and
enjoy the view before heading down. The view from the top was absolutely
breathtaking, with panoramic views of the surrounding mountains and valleys.

![Us at the peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_hoellental/2025-09-18-14.jpg)

I have to mention here that there are several things to take into consideration
before going for the summit. First, the weather can change rapidly in the
mountains, so the valley below can be sunny while the summit is covered in
clouds, or vice versa. Second, the summit and the cross,
which was recently [taken down for renovation](https://www.berliner-kurier.de/panorama/sticker-wahnsinn-auf-der-zugspitze-gipfelkreuz-muss-in-reparatur-li.10005143),
are quite crowded with tourists who take the cable car up. This can make it
hard to find a quiet spot to enjoy the view or take the obligatory summit
photos.

After asking some girls in city-style sneakers for a quick photo of us at the
summit, we headed to the station, bought our ticket (44 Euros now, including
the train to Garmisch!), and took the cable car down to the valley. David was
enjoying the view and the ride, while I almost teared up thinking about what
a great adventure this hike was. It might have been the pressure change that
induced the emotional response though, I'm a tough man after all.

![Compilation of the road to Zugspitze (2962 m) over Höllental](/404/)

Anyway, the route is a must-go for everyone with proper equipment and some
experience behind them. I would say the glacier is the part where the equipment
is most necessary, while the via ferrata sections are the most challenging
parts.

![Full first via ferrata climb](/404/)

If you're not time-limited, I would recommend either staying overnight at
Höllentalangerhütte or going down to Knorrhütte and spending the next night
there. This way, you can split the hike into two days, take your time and
enjoy the scenery more. Just make sure to book your stay in advance, as these
huts can get quite busy during the hiking season.

![Full main via ferrata climb](/404/)

P.S. Feels amazing to have finally completed the Zugspitze (2962 m) ascent via both main
routes! Looking forward to Jubiläumsgrat and the Wiener-Neustädter hut
hopefully next year.
