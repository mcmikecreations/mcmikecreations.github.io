The first time I visited [Buchstein (1701 m)](/hikes/2025-05-11-buchstein/),
the idea of filming the climb to
the top of the rock kept nagging me. With the weather getting worse each day,
I decided to risk it and take the shorter route up Buchstein with the goal of
recording the summit ascent. To differentiate this hike from the other version,
I decided to hide away my map and only follow the signs.

![Bergwacht at the start](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-00.jpg)

The public transport to Bayerwald operates twice on Sunday, so an early morning
alarm was unavoidable. After getting to the bus stop (hopping off the regional
train in Tegernsee), I quickly realized how bad the temperature has gotten and
how risky the climb can be if the stones are wet.

![Route up the mountain](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-01.jpg)

Still, I decided to at least check out the conditions at the top, so I started
the hike. The first section to Sonnberg Alm Niederleger is a normal mountain
path, a bit slippery in some parts. At some point along the route, I turned
back to see the view and saw a snowstorm approaching in the distance.

![Buchstein (1701 m) and Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-02.jpg)

It was still far away, so I made a decision to push through to the top. I kept
going to Sonnberg Alm Hochleger, noticing the road became more rocky, muddy and
slippery. I ignored those signs and kept going to reach Tegernseer Hütte at
least to see Achensee from the distance.

![The approaching snowstorm with barely visible Achensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-03.jpg)

Standing at the bottom of the rocks, I decided to take the shorter route. To my
surprise, it already required climbing equipment and was fairly steep, with a
metal rope to hold/clip onto. Since I conveniently left my equipment (besides
the camera) at home, I had to rely on my hands to ensure my safety.

![View from Buchstein (1701 m) onto Roßsteinalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-05.jpg)

Nearly reaching the hut at the dip between the peaks of the rock, I saw a few
other people climbing Buchstein (1701 m) to reach the summit. This gave me
enough confidence to keep going, despite tiny drops of rain and snow already
reaching me for half an hour.

![View from Buchstein (1701 m) onto Tegernseer Hütte and Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-04.jpg)

I roughly remembered the way to the top from last time, meaning which holes and
bumps in the rock to grab or climb, so going to the top wasn't an issue. I took
the visitor book to put my name in there, not paying much attention to my
surroundings.

![View from Tegernseer Hütte down below during the snowstorm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-08.jpg)

When I finally put away the book and looked at the scenery, I saw the snowstorm
being much closer, maybe one valley away from my position. I scrambled to climb
down, but the rocks were already wet and slippery, so I had to take my time
(also I tend to climb slowly because of caution bordering on fear).

![Coffee and pineapple-coconut cake](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-06.jpg)
![Historical photos at Tegernseer Hütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-07.jpg)
![Getting ready to smash my phone](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-09.jpg)

Being showered with icicles, I reached the hut, got some coffee and cake to
calm, warm up, and reward myself, and headed down to Tegernsee.

![Milk-cap mushroom](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-10.jpg)
![Metal shelter at 47.643754, 11.692640](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-11.jpg)

Going down the presumed path in the direction of Tegernsee and getting lost a
few times (no maps, remember?), I managed to reach Leonhardstein (1452 m). I
had an idea to climb it too, but it was already too slippery and I chose to
head back to civilization instead.

![Leonhardstein (1452 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-12.jpg)
![Fallen sycamore maple leaf](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-13.jpg)

Reaching Kreuth, the fancy bus stop with an E-ink display notified me that the
bus will arrive in an hour. Freezing on the spot, I had to keep going all the
way to Scharling (where I ended my hike to [Hirschberg (1668 m)](/404/)) to
warm myself up, where the bus finally caught up with me and I headed home.

![View onto Kreuth near 47.641778, 11.734758](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-14.jpg)
![Compilation of the Buchstein (1701 m) hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/buchstein_only/2025-10-05-15.mp4)

![The climb itself](/404/)

In general, if you don't mind the early public transport journey, I would
recommend this route over the other much longer version I did before. The views
are worth it and the rock climb at the top is optional. The hut itself was also
nice, with a possibility to stay overnight, if one chooses to.

P.S. For correct map routing over via ferrata on
[OpenRouteService](https://maps.openrouteservice.org/),
a custom build of the service
[is required](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/t5-t6-ferrata-ignored/2622/12).
