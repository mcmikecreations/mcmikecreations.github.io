David from my [Brauneck (1555 m) Bonfire Celebration](/hikes/2026-06-27-brauneck_only/)
messaged me saying he borrowed a drone and wanted to get some cool shots in the
allowed zones. After looking at my to-do list of peaks and the zoning map, we
settled on the Laubeneck (1758 m) peak, close to the
[Teufelstättkopf (1758 m) loop](/hikes/2026-05-10-teufelstaettkopf/)
that I recently did with Bohdan.

![The fountain at Castle Linderhof](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-00.jpg)

The start of the hike was really not that great. When I went to
[Große Klammspitze (1924 m)](/404/),
I took the train to Garmisch and switched to a bus straight to Linderhof. This
time, we travelled to Füssen, changed to Reutte in Austria (paying 6.30 Euro
for a bus ticket), changed again to Linderhof. For every change we waited an
hour. Still, eventually we reached the Linderhof castle and started our hike.

![The start of the trail near Brunnenkopfhütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-01.jpg)
![View from the trail towards Große Klammspitze (1924 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-03.jpg)

This route starts off light, with a normal dirt road leading to
Brunnenkopfhütte. Shortly before the hut, we made a sharp turn east and took
the trail towards Unterammergau, reconnecting with my
[older hike to retrieve a lost hiking stick](/hikes/2026-03-22-unterammergau_sonnenberg/)
near August-Schuster-Haus.

![View from Dreisäuler Kopf (1545 m) towards Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-02.jpg)
![Dreisäuler Kopf (1545 m) panorama, filmed by [David Bilan](https://bilandavid.com/)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-17.mp4 "autoplay")

The first peak along the way, Dreisäuler Kopf (1545 m), is basically
unreachable on foot without climbing. The trail goes far below the summit,
requiring you to go straight uphill on slippery grass growing on dusty soil.
There is no cross and no view on the top to reward you too, so we decided to
just skip the challenge and keep going.

![Fly-by between Dreisäuler Kopf (1545 m) and Hennen-Kopf (1796 m), filmed by [David Bilan](https://bilandavid.com/)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-18.mp4 "autoplay")
![The cliffs at Hennen-Kopf (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-04.jpg)
![Look at Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) and Zugspitze (2962 m) from the air](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-20.jpg)

The next target, Hennen-Kopf (1796 m), is also tough to reach, but at least
there's a trail leading to a cross. It's hard to find it though, because the
signs showing the route are quite well hidden. Regardless, we enjoyed the view
towards Linderhof and [Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2026-07-19-schneefernerkopf/)
for a bit and kept walking up. We even walked past some other hikers going from
Ammergau towards Linderhof (although seemingly skipping the peaks).

![Field Scabious on the Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-05.jpg)
![Mykola aura-farming near Hennen-Kopf (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-06.jpg)
![Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) panorama, filmed by [David Bilan](https://bilandavid.com/)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-19.mp4 "autoplay")

The Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) summit itself is quite cool. It's a multi-rock
formation with trails or climbing routes going up each one. We went up the
biggest one, greeted by a nice cross at the very top. Although we were already
a bit pressed for time due to the commute, we stayed there to take some photos
and such, since we wanted the journey up to pay off.

![The team posing at Hennen-Kopf (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-21.jpg)
![Alpine salamander on the trail near Laubeneck (1520 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-07.jpg)
![View from Laubeneck (1520 m) towards Teufelstättkopf (1758 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-08.jpg)

After going back onto the main trail, we kept going towards Laubeneck (1520 m).
It's another tricky summit to easily miss, since the trail going there is quite
narrow and covered in spruce bushes. At this point, the team started worrying
about the arrival time and the hike duration, but we were already far from both
ends of the hike, meaning they had no choice but to keep going.

![Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) from the air above Laubeneck (1520 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-22.jpg)
![The team on Laubeneck (1520 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-09.jpg)

This peak itself is also quite nice, although noticeably lower than the last
one. I remember seeing it from [Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/)
and wondering how to even get there. Turns out, the other side of the mountain
is much more shallow and walkable (in summer), so going up and down isn't much
of an issue.

![Taking a stroll on Laubeneck (1520 m), filmed by [David Bilan](https://bilandavid.com/)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-23.mp4 "autoplay")
![David and Dmytro descending from Laubeneck (1520 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-10.jpg)

The view from the top isn't much different from the Teufelstättkopf (1758 m)
panorama, but it was a nice addition to my collection. Around this point the
sun hid behind the clouds, the wind picked up, and the temperature dropped to
roughly 14 degrees, making us put on our jackets. On the upside, the
[Höllental side of Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/)
revealed itself from behind the clouds and we could enjoy the ridge in full
glory.

![The team going towards Pürschlinghaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-11.jpg)
![The saddle near Latschenkopf (1634 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-12.jpg)

From here onwards, the interesting part of the hike was done, and we started
walking down towards Unterammergau. We passed the saddle near
Latschenkopf (1634 m) quite quickly, with the team complaining about additional
elevation gain on the way. At August-Schuster-Haus (Pürschlinghaus) we were
blocked by some cows, but they stepped off the road when they saw us coming.

![View from the Latschenkopf (1634 m) saddle towards Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-13.jpg)
![Cows near Pürschlinghaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-14.jpg)

Since then, we just walked on the gravel/asphalt/dirt road down all the way
to the town. Around the parking lot exiting the slope, we called a local pizza
place and ordered food we could enjoy while waiting for an SEV bus to
[Murnau](/404/).

![Asphalt road down from August-Schuster-Haus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-15.jpg)
![Sunset over Teufelstättkopf (1758 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/laubeneck/2026-07-26-16.jpg)

All in all, it's an interesting route between a nice castle and a cozy town,
but the commute almost makes it not worth the effort. The views between
Sonnenberg (1622 m), Latschenkopf (1634 m), Laubeneck (1520 m), and
Hennen-Kopf (1796 m) are largely the same and show you the valley leading
towards Zugspitze (2962 m). I would say the [Teufelstättkopf (1758 m) loop](/hikes/2026-05-10-teufelstaettkopf/)
is the more appealing choice if you want to repeat the route. For me personally
it was a memorable event due to the drone shots we made, but as a plain walk
it's not as exciting.
