After my November hike to [Hirschberg (1668 m)](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/),
I was looking for adventures. Mikuláš and I were eyeing Signalkopf (1895 m) as
a potential hike for a really long time. Given the season and the unknown snow
conditions, we decided to take the highest peak first and see how it goes. The
peak in question is the highest point of the ridge, Soiernspitze (2253 m).

![Aschauer Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-00.jpg)

I asked my flatmates if they wanted to join, and Felix was up for it. We took
the train and the bus to the barracks near Mittenwald and started our hike.
The first section led us through the forest on a well-marked dirt road. We were
debating if we should take the southern trail across the river or the northern
one up the slope. I double-checked the tags on [openstreetmap.org](https://www.openstreetmap.org/)
and saw no bridge across the river, so to be safe, we took the northern trail.

![Road near Ochsenalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-01.jpg)

As we got closer to Krinner-Kofler-Hütte (1407 m), we saw a big group of
mountain goats grazing on the slope. They saw us from afar and started moving
away, so we didn't get to snap any good photos.

![Approaching Vereiner Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-02.jpg)

Since the road to Vereiner Alm is located in a valley between two ridges, the
whole area is usually covered in shade and fog. Luckily for us, the weather was
clear that day, so the only thing we had to deal with was the snow that didn't
melt in the dark shade of the mountains.

Soon enough, we walked past the crossing between our current trail and the
southern trail we considered earlier. There were no signs of a bridge and the
snow was untouched and waist-high, so we were glad we took the northern route.

![Starting the ascent](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-03.jpg)

We reached the start of the mountain slope leading to our destination and
started our ascent. The snow was still quite deep, so Mikuláš took charge with
his gaiters to break the trail. Felix and I followed closely behind, with Felix
wearing his outdoor shoes not designed for deep snow. We made steady progress,
taking breaks as needed to catch our breath and enjoy the surrounding views.

The sun came out as we ascended higher, illuminating the snow-covered landscape
and creating a beautiful contrast against the blue sky. The snow was getting
softer and shallower as we climbed, making it easier to walk without sinking
too deep.

![View from the Jöchel mountain pass (1787 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-04.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the Jöchel mountain pass (1787 m), where we took a
longer break to refuel and prepare for the final climb to the top. Felix was
feeling less confident about continuing, and I was starting to feel tired from
the steep slope. Mikuláš encouraged us to pay attention to our feelings and
body and make the best decision for ourselves.

![Looking down at Hirzeneck (1803 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-05.jpg)

We climbed a bit higher, but after some consideration, Felix decided to turn
back and walk down to Vereiner Alm and travel back home. Mikuláš told me to
keep going if I felt up to it, while he would bring Felix down safely first.
I managed to slip and hurt my arm pretty badly on a rock, but I was determined
to keep going.

![The climb to the Soiernspitze (2253 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-06.jpg)

The terrain from that point was more rocky than snowy, so I was able to
continue with less trouble. But I started to worry about the descent, as it was
going to be steep and slippery. I took my time, using my trekking poles for
stability and carefully choosing my footing.

![Crawling to the cross at Soiernspitze (2253 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-12.jpg)

Somehow, Mikuláš managed to walk Felix down and catch up with me before the
summit. We continued together, with Mikuláš leading the way and breaking the
trail where needed. Finally, we reached the summit of Soiernspitze (2253 m),
where we were rewarded with breathtaking panoramic views of the surrounding
mountains and valleys. The snow was much deeper at the top than I expected
despite the wind and sun exposure. At that point I was so tired that I could
barely stand, I'm not sure why the altitude affected me that much.

![The snow level at Soiernspitze (2253 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-07.jpg)
![View from Soiernspitze (2253 m) onto Walchensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-08.jpg)

The iced-over snow on the entire ridge made it very risky to continue towards
Signalkopf (1895 m), so we decided to call it a day and return in summer to
complete the ridge hike. It was better than risking injury or death on the way.

![The cross at Soiernspitze (2253 m) with Krapfenkarspitze (2110 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-09.jpg)

We took our time to soak in the scenery and snap photos in every direction, and
while doing so, we noticed a few other hikers walking in our direction from
Feldernkopf (2071 m). I'm not sure if we had an inner need to wait for them or
if we just wanted to enjoy the summit a bit longer, but we stayed put until
they reached us. It was a couple from Munich who decided to hike the entire
ridge like we initially planned.

![The view at the Soierngruppe ridge with a few pixels denoting the hiker couple](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-10.jpg)

The girl managed to fall on the rocks while going along the ridge, as steep
terrain made it very hard to keep balance, and there were no safety ropes or
rocks to hold onto. They were asking for painkillers, since they didn't have
DAV insurance to call for rescue. Unfortunately for them, we didn't have any
either, so we could only offer moral support and help them get down safely.

![I started the descent](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-11.jpg)

The descent was indeed tricky, with all of us slipping and sliding on the loose
rocks and snow patches. We took it slow and steady, using our trekking poles
for balance and helping each other where needed. As we descended to the pass
and saw the huts below, we felt a sense of relief and accomplishment. Although
the condition of the girl was concerning, we knew that from that point on, the
terrain would be easier to navigate.

![The sun near the neighboring mountains on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-13.jpg)

We broke the trail for the couple and headed down to the hut. Since I don't
have gaiters myself, I was struggling with water and snow getting into my
shoes. Still, it was manageable, and I was doing my part to help the couple
down safely. AFter reaching the hut and changing our socks, we continued down
the relatively clear dirt road back to the barracks. The couple told us they
were grateful for our help and company, and would manage the rest of the way on
their own.

![Mykola (on the right) descending while ignoring the sunset](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-14.jpg)

The sun at that point has already set, and we were walking in the dusk. It was
amazing to see all the colors in the sky and the mountains as we made our way
back. Soon, the stars started to appear, and we felt a sense of wonder and awe
at the beauty of nature. There were no streetlights or artificial lights
around, and the sky was so clear that we could see the Milky Way stretching
across the sky. You don't get to see that often in the city!

![The last rays of sun near the Jöchel mountain pass (1787 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/soiernspitze/2025-01-19-15.jpg)

At the end, we reached the barracks and waited for the last bus back to
Mittenwald. Guess what? The bus actually goes on the other side of the road,
and that absolute dickhead driver didn't even bother to stop for us! We had to
walk all the way back to Mittenwald on foot, which took us another hour in the
dark and cold of the night.

To sum up, this hike is manageable in any season with the right gear, but I
would advise against going alone or walking on the ridge in winter. Although
the ridge "trail" is not marked as closed in winter, the conditions are just
too dangerous without a full set of alpine gear and experience. Repeating this
trip in summer would be much more enjoyable and safer, allowing for a full
exploration of the ridge between Soiernspitze (2253 m) and Signalkopf (1895 m).
