After my [trip to Landshut](/hikes/2026-04-03-moosburg_landshut/) that took
around 30 kilometers, I was ready to do an easier hike. Thus, I was looking
for a group that would focus on socializing and not just speeding through the
summits.

![Bad Wiessee near river Söllbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-00.jpg)

Natalia from "Wandern für glückliche Menschen" wanted to organize a trip to
Schrecksee that I myself planned for two years, but after I warned her about
the snow, she changed the route to visit Fockenstein (1564 m) instead. Although
I [already visited the peak](/404/) with Maskym, it was better than sitting at
home, so I decided to accept the invite.

![Coming up to Sonnenbichl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-01.jpg)
![Liverleaf near the Sonnenbichl hotel](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-02.jpg)

We started by walking to the Sonnenbichl hotel. I proposed taking the steeper
ski slope up to visit Zwergelberg (1113 m) instead of taking the forest trail
which I walked on in the past. The group was not too thrilled, but after taking
a toilet break, we put our differences aside and set off.

![The ski slope near hotel Sonnenbichl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-03.jpg)
![Coltsfoot on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-04.jpg)

After climbing the slope, we approached the Zwergelberg (1113 m) summit, but
the group decided to skip it and keep walking. Natalia and I made a detour to
actually check the peak out, seeing an electric pole and some metal poles in
the ground.

![The mechanism at the top of the ski slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-05.jpg)
![Zwergelberg (1113 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-06.jpg)

As we kept going, the group started suggesting that we should make a picnic
break, as most people were getting hungry. Although I'm used to ignoring my
hunger and thirst to get home quicker, the others wanted to make this trip slow
and enjoyable, so we did the break and ate the snacks we had.

![A view towards Ochsenkamp (1594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-07.jpg)
![Neuhüttenalm near Fockenstein (1564 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-08.jpg)

Eventually we reached Aueralm (1269 m), where some hikers took another toilet
break. We managed to convince them to keep going though, instead of taking a
longer break to eat a meal at the Alpine restaurant. Soon after, we went to
Neuhüttenalm near Fockenstein (1564 m), where I waited for the rest of the
group to catch up, then climbed up Fockenstein (1564 m) itself.

![View from Fockenstein (1564 m) towards Auerkamp (1607 m) and Hirschberg (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-09.jpg)
![The Fockenstein (1564 m) summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-10.jpg)

The whole way through, the snow was melting from the 20 degree heat, and there
were giant puddles everywhere. We were sliding down all the time, but it was
nothing compared to my [hike to Auerkamp (1607 m)](/hikes/2026-03-29-lenggries_auerkamp/)
where I had to climb up the snow with my hands. When I and two others got to
the top, the rest of the group was nowhere to be seen, so we started taking
the photos and drone shots alone.

![The Ukrainian group at Fockenstein (1564 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-11.jpg)
![A warning sign near Fockenstein (1564 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-12.jpg)

It was really nice seeing [Hirschberg (1670 m)](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/)
from the summit, which brought back a lot of memories of the DAAD FK group
going there. After the rest of the group showed up 30 minutes later, Natalia
asked the other hikers at the cross to take photos of our group, and one of
our member took some drone shots as additional group photos.

![A group member descending Fockenstein (1564 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-13.jpg)

Once we started descending 1.5 hours after reaching the Fockenstein (1564 m)
summit, I realized the trip was going too slow for my taste and left to visit
a few more peaks. First, I followed some random footsteps to
Schlagkopf (1330 m), where I discovered a tower of rocks instead of a cross.

![The summit of Schlagkopf (1330 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-14.jpg)
![A hunting stand between Schlagkopf (1330 m) and Geierstein (1491 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-15.jpg)

I kept going all the way down to the hunting stand on the crossroads, then took
the turn to Geierstein (1491 m). On the way there, I came across a dried-up
avalanche which I had to climb over. This was the second time I had to traverse
one, the first one being in Austria on the way down from
[Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m)](/hikes/2026-03-06-hoher_kalmberg/).

![An old avalanche near Geierstein (1491 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-16.jpg)
![Coming up to Geierstein (1491 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-17.jpg)

As I was reaching the cliffside peak, I came across two guys going down. They
warned me it took them more than 3 hours to climb from Lenggries to the top of
Geierstein (1491 m), so my descent should take around 2 hours at least. My
app said 1.5 hours, and I was confident I could lower it down to 1 hour, but I
still thanked them for the feedback.

![The view north from Geierstein (1491 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-18.jpg)
![The cross at Geierstein (1491 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-19.jpg)

As I reached the summit, there was nobody there, and the sunset was in full
force. I didn't linger for too long and went down to another viewing spot
to see a better panorama of Lenggries and [Benediktenwand (1800 m)](/hikes/2024-07-21-benediktenwand/),
where I spent some time to catch my breath. I messaged the group to say that
I should be down in Lenggries in about 1.5 hours, but didn't hear anything
back from them, assuming they would wait for me.

![The view from Geierstein (1491 m) towards Lenggries](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-20.jpg)
![The sunset near Geierstein (1491 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-21.jpg)

As I started climbing down, I realized why it took so long for the hikers to
reach the top. The route was very steep, covered in snow, ice, mud, and loose
rocks. I was staying on the cautious side, making sure my every step was solid.
Still, I made it down the steeper area pretty fast, took off my gaiters and
shoe spikes, and continued on foot.

![The sunset with a view of Lenggries](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-22.jpg)
![Cowslip on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-23.jpg)

The more I descended, the easier and more horizontal the way became. At the end
I reached the Hohenburger Weiher lake, with some locals walking their dogs
around the lake. I followed the hiking trail to the train station, then skipped
two trains as I was waiting for more than an hour for the rest of the group to
catch up, although they were taking the much faster dirt road straight from
Fockenstein (1564 m) to Lenggries.

![Hohenburger Weiher lake](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fockenstein_geierstein/2026-04-05-24.jpg)

Overall, I would certainly recommend this route for medium-level hikers. It's
decently challenging and you can see great views all around. The first half up
to Fockenstein (1564 m) gives you a view of Tegernsee, Hirschberg (1670 m), and
Ochsenkamp (1594 m). The second half shows a great panorama of Lenggries and
Brauneck (1555 m), with barely visible Benediktenwand (1800 m) in the back.
That being said, this route is best attempted in summer, as climbing the
rocky Geierstein (1491 m) may prove dangerous with snow and ice around.
