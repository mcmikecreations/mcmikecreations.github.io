After the semi-successful hike to [Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/)
where I lost my hiking
pole, I was on the lookout for a sunny weekend to go back and retrieve it.
I've also monitored Kleinanzeigen for the exact same model, but only found
a few overpriced offers. Although the weather seemed ok at first, it
drastically changed on Saturday, with strong north-facing winds and 8 cm of
snow. Remembering my previous struggles on the ridge, I had to fall back to
this Blombergbahn route instead.

![A house in Bichl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-00.jpg)

Ever since I went sledging near the Blombergbahn with David Bilan, I was
excited to visit the surrounding peaks, although I haven't prepared much by
looking at the map and other peoples' routes. I had a plan to visit everything
on the ridge, then come down to the bus station near Blombergbahn and head
home. But fate had other plans for me...

![Approaching the mountains](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-01.jpg)
![The trail towards Hollerkopf (932 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-02.jpg)

The beginning of the hike was a walk through the forest. Tall pines and light
snow reinforced my belief that I did the right choice. Higher up, around 1200 m
of elevation, the whole area was covered in ankle-deep snow.

![The top of Hollerkopf (932 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-03.jpg)
![Frozen pines on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-04.jpg)

My first checkpoint on the hike was Hollerkopf (932 m). While walking along a
wide dirt road onto the hillside, there was a detour uphill with deep muddy
tractor tracks. I instinctively knew that they led to the peak. Following them
and getting my shoes dirty, I pushed to the top and got there soon after. There
was no cross and no marks there, just a barely visible unmarked trail and some
metal poles sticking from the ground denoting some administrative borders.

![A hunting stand on the way to Enzenauer Kopf (1205 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-05.jpg)

I traced my steps back onto the bigger road, and continued onwards. Although I
noticed a few footsteps in the snow along the road and came across a few huts
and hunting stands, there were no people to be seen, so I just kept walking.
I did see some rabbit and goat footsteps though, making me feel like a hunter
out for some game.

![The top of Enzenauer Kopf (1205 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-06.jpg)
![A trough near the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-07.jpg)

Soon after, I approached Enzenauer Kopf (1205 m) and decided to simply take
the direct way uphill instead of following the road. Climbing up past some
bushes and young pines made me question my decision, since although I did
my best to avoid damaging the nature, I wasn't sure I pulled it off completely.
In any case, the peak showed itself to me soon after, again with no cross
(and the highest point on the map was actually down below in the bushes on
the other side of the hill), and I turned back onto the main road onwards.

![The supposed path towards Fahrtkopf (1203 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-08.jpg)
![The top of Fahrtkopf (1203 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-09.jpg)

My next destination was Fahrtkopf (1203 m). I tried to follow the GPS, since
the trail wasn't visible under the snow and I didn't see any markings either,
but very quickly I got lost. In reality, the trail was there, but there were
other trails and dried streams one could follow to get to the same place,
so I just winged it and hiked in the general direction of the next peak.

![A sign on the way down from Fahrtkopf (1203 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-10.jpg)

After reaching a bigger road and turning off of it to get to the peak again,
I was greeted with a hilltop covered in artificially planted young pines
overgrown with spiky wines. Considering my previous fall at
[Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m)](/hikes/2025-11-23-vordere_kesselschneid/) and
[Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/),
I didn't want to risk damaging my jacket and pants any further, so I tried
my best to either walk around or fully step on the wines. While this worked,
there was again no mark at the top, so I had to aimlessly wander around looking
for the actual highest point so that I can claim I reached the peak.

![The top of Angerlkopf (1249 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-11.jpg)
![A farmer's hut near Angerlkopf (1249 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-12.jpg)

After this confusing detour, I came back to the road and noticed that the 
easiest way to the next peak is straight up. The barely visible trail was
overgrown with planted pines again, so I just climbed up the hill on the
edge of the plant area, joining the trail closer to the top. The peak itself,
Angerlkopf (1249 m), was yet again without markings (or they were covered in
snow), but at least it was an open area and there were multiple footsteps from
other hikers trying to figure out where the highest point is.

![A crossroads near Stallauer Kopf (1322 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-13.jpg)
![The supposed peak of Stallauer Kopf (1322 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-14.jpg)

Some of the footsteps around were fresh, and I followed them along the ridge
to the next peak on the route, Stallauer Kopf (1322 m). Now this can hardly
be called a peak. It's basically a crossing of several trails connecting
surrounding mountains and forest roads, with signs everywhere pointing in
all directions. I didn't overstay my welcome and hurried to the next target.

![The real peak of Stallauer Eck (1213 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-15.jpg)

The route to Stallauer Eck (1213 m) completely changed the mood of the hike.
Immediately after leaving the crossing, I met an elderly couple hiking from
Stallauer Eck (1213 m) towards Blombergbahn. Soon after, I came across a
trail runner. Then came a mom with two young sons, then a father with an
infant in his backpack. I was overflowing with joy to see actual people
after following footsteps alone for several hours.

![A horsefly trap near the top of Stallauer Eck (1213 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-16.jpg)
![The view on the way back](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-17.jpg)

When I reached Stallauer Eck (1213 m), there was a proper cross with a sign
and some benches there, and some people enjoying a break and talking about
their previous hiking experiences. I greeted them, but couldn't stay long,
realizing the long way left ahead of me. When I turned around and started
going back to Stallauer Kopf (1322 m), I started catching up with everyone
I met before (in reverse order, of course).

![A small cross on the way towards Zwiesel (1348 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-18.jpg)
![The peak of Zwiesel (1348 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-19.jpg)

The kids were excited to see me again, the father gestured me to stay silent
near his infant sleeping in the backpack, and the elderly couple wanted to
rest and chat for a bit, but I apologised and kept going. The trail to
Zwiesel (1348 m) was very easy and well-marked with footsteps from other
people. But I had to stay alert, because the snow there was already deeper
than before, and sinking into it meant I'd get it inside my shoes and pants
for certain.

![The view from Zwiesel (1348 m) towards Blombergbahn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-20.jpg)

The Zwiesel (1348 m) peak itself was the most exciting one in the area. The
view from the top covers all surrounding mountains, there are no trees there
to cover the sight, and I could see all my targets from there. Although
initially I aimed to go to Blomberg Westgipfel (1248 m) next, something
prompted me to keep going straight down in the direction of Heiglkopf (1218 m)
instead.

![The muddy trail down from Zwiesel (1348 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-21.jpg)
![The road along river Großbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-22.jpg)

I don't know how the descent looks like in summer with dry weather, but at this
time of the year, the whole slope is muddy and slippery, which slowed my down
to a crawl. It took quite a while to reach the Großbach river at the bottom of
the hill, and it took even longer to climb onto the next mountain. There was a
barely visible steep dirt track, probably laid out to carry cut down trees back
to the town. I didn't want to take the long way around to the official trail
up Heiglkopf (1218 m), so I went on the dirt track instead.

![The forestry road up Heiglkopf (1218 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-23.jpg)
![A butterbur growing on the road near Heiglkopf (1218 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-24.jpg)

The route was great, with some slight overgrowth here and there, but totally
safe to traverse. There were a few turns here and there, and I was choosing
random directions trying to keep walking uphill. Eventually, I reached the
highest point on the road and went off to just climb up the slope. This proved
to be difficult given the barbed wire on the fence, but after some twiddling
around I found a weaker spot and climbed under the wire, managing to not
scratch myself or tear my clothes.

![The older hiking couple living near Heiglkopf (1218 m) going uphill](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-25.jpg)
![The summit of Heiglkopf (1218 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-26.jpg)

From there on out, it was a normal hike to the top. There were tons of people
on the rocky road, mostly adults and elderly hikers. I saw a shortcut on the
map and tried following it, but the road was closed to preserve local nature,
so I had to rejoin the main trail. There I met an older local couple hiking
up Heiglkopf (1218 m) and joined them. They were very fast and I barely managed
to keep up. When we reached the top, they told me they had a house at the base
of the mountain and hike it multiple times a week to walk their dog, so this
was just an evening walk for them.

![The Blombergkreuz on the way to the actual mountaintop](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-27.jpg)
![The viewing platform at Blomberg Ostgipfel (1237 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-28.jpg)

I paid my farewell and continued onwards to Blomberg Ostgipfel (1237 m), which
had a viewing platform but no cross. I didn't linger there and didn't pay for
the binoculars, instead walking further past the cable car and towards the
restaurant (Berggasthof Blomberghaus).

![The advertisements at the Blombergbahn top station (not sponsored)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-29.jpg)
![One of the lion statues at Berggasthof Blomberghaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-30.jpg)

I also wanted to keep going to Blomberg Westgipfel (1248 m), but apparently the
route to the top was a part of privately owned land and I had to skip it. Later
on, I checked the heatmap on Strava and saw that some people did manage to
climb over the fence and reach the top.

![The way down from Berggasthof Blomberghaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-31.jpg)

After a quick meal at the restaurant, I went downhill to the base of the cable
car, then walked to Bad Tölz and boarded the train. It felt weird walking
through the tourist-filled town being covered in mud and sweat, while the
visitors were strolling along the streets taking photos of the architecture,
occasionally having me in the picture.

![Walking through Bad Tölz](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bichl_blomberg/2026-03-15-32.jpg)

Overall, this is a great area for hiking, especially for beginners. I wouldn't
recommend following the track I took, as there wasn't much to see on most of
the "peaks", but otherwise hiking up Heiglkopf (1218 m), Zwiesel (1348 m),
or Blomberg Ostgipfel (1237 m) is a great way to spend a day. The routes are
always filled with hikers and the restaurant at the top is open most of the
year!
