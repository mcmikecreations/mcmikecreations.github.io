Straight after [last week's Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/hikes/2026-08-02-baumgartenjoch/)
and the dentist visit thereafter, I went on vacation to Croatia. The road took
me 17 hours by FlixBus and only made the muscle pain worse, so I took some days
off adventuring to recover and prepare for new peaks.

![Flovers on the streets of Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-00.jpg)

Since I ended up visiting my mom vacationing in Omiš again, I decided to
explore other mountains in the area of the [Old Fort via ferrata](/hikes/2025-08-14-omis_fort_climb/).
I wasn't sure about the trail conditions of the higher mountains (and had
some negative experiences in the past), so it made sense to take a shorter
route first and do some scouting.

![Rocky climb at the start of the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-01.jpg)

I looked at the map in my [LigMapper app](/404/) and found Čeline (455 m), a
local mountain overseeing Duće with a nearby church and some power lines. The
official route is part of the "Stare Duće - Gospa od Sniga" trail, but I took
a detour to visit another peak nearby - Babnjača (334 m).

![Steep climb on the rocks towards the cliffs](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-02.jpg)

The route starts at the old Omiš bus stop, with a random trail leading under
the cliff too. Straight from the start, hikers are greeted with a nicely-marked
but rocky and steep climb, where one has to jump between rocks and avoid
sliding down on gravel.

![Cephalaria growing on the rocks](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-03.jpg)

The surrounding forest provides enough shade, but with 37 degrees outside, no
amount of shade can hide you from dehydration. As I kept climbing, I was
surrounded by cliffs from both sides, leaving Omiš as the only view to look at.

![View from the climb towards Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-04.jpg)

After quite a lot of climbing, I entered a plateau closer to the top of the
mountains. There, the rocky path ends and you enter a steppe with tall grass
and spiky ferns. A few minutes of walking later, I was scratched by spikes
from head to toe. Still, this was just the beginning of the hike, and I had two
summits in front of me.

![The steppe between the mountain ridges](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-05.jpg)

The road from the plains splits a couple of times. I didn't really get why back
then. I don't fully grasp it now either, but I've since learned that there used
to be rock climbing routes nearby, so some of the trails probably lead there.
Moreover, the local tourist board seems to take a safe and cheap approach with
hiking routes - unless the trail is super popular like the [Old Fort hike](/hikes/2025-08-13-omis_fort/),
the curious tourists shouldn't go there, so there's no point maintaining it.
And the more experienced hikers (like me of course) would still find a way.

Although I wasn't sure I'd find any routes leading to Babnjača (334 m), I
saw something resembling a path deep in the shrubs, and took my chances.
Climbing through the bushes and pricking myself on the thorns, I got through
the natural barricade and stepped foot on a real old trail. I was happy I
wasn't hallucinating a track, but nobody used it for ages and I still debated
myself if I should stay on it or turn back.

![The view from the Babnjača (334 m) ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-06.jpg)

My thrill to visit an [extra peak](/hikes/tag/Web/) got the better of me, and
I took my chances. The trail wasn't actually that bad, and I drew blood only
a couple of times fighting my way through the plants. When I reached the
ridge, the trail abruptly ended on some rocks. With no markings to be seen,
the rest of the way was improvised, which you can see in the GPX file.

On the flipside, the ridge opened up to a nice panorama of Omiš,
Čeline (455 m), Sveti Jure (1319 m), and Stražina (731 m). It was a bit tricky
to traverse the rocks and find stable footing, but I kept going and reached
Babnjača (334 m) soon after. I don't know what I expected, but the peak had
nothing on it. No cross, no log book, no markings, no traces of human
existence. I did a small loop around the peak to see if I was at least missing
some trash left between the rocks, but there was nothing at all.

![The view from Babnjača (334 m) towards Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-07.jpg)

The way back was roughly the same. I felt more comfortable and walked faster on
the rocks, knowing none of them were shaky or slippery. The GPS in my watch was
spazzing out, so I missed the trail I took on the way up, and climbed down a
steeper and spikier way, getting some deep cuts on my fingers. When I reached
the original trail, I was overjoyed. The stupid detour was over and I could
continue on the real path ahead.

![The steppe trail between Babnjača (334 m) and Čeline (455 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-20.mp4 "autoplay")

The rest of the way to the Church of Our Lady of the Snows was nice, with the
road in a decent shape, and the sun setting on the horizon. I tried going
faster on this easier section expecting to see more trouble ahead, and soon
after I faced the entrance to the church area.

![The stone ruins/bunker near the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-08.jpg)

As you'd expect, the first thing you see are the power lines with their massive
steel towers going up in the sky. If you pay attention, right underneath you'll
see old stone bunkers and technical "buildings" that litter the cliffs and
mountains of Omiš. Walking past them, you enter a flat area with a big cross
you can see from the shore, a flag of Croatia and the Crkva Gospe Snježne
itself.

![The front of the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-09.jpg)
![The climb from the church towards Čeline (455 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-10.jpg)

The view from there is nice, but it's located in a saddle between two larger
mountains, so you don't get a full panoramic view. I heard some voices of other
hikers and decided to keep going. Walking past the church, I noted some benches
on the back side, and started my climb.

![The view from Čeline (455 m) towards Zahod (594 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-11.jpg)
![The climb down from Čeline (455 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-12.jpg)

The way up Čeline (455 m) was not much better than the detour. Although it's a
bit easier to find the trail where one can be found, the rocks and ferns still
pose some challenge. The Čeline (455 m) peak itself is no different from
Babnjača (334 m), offering largely the same views and not being marked in any
way. With the popular hiking trail to the church, this one at least gets some
foot traffic from time to time.

![The Croatian flag and the big cross near the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-13.jpg)

I offered the official popular section of this trail to my mom (she completed
the [Old Fort hike](/hikes/2025-08-13-omis_fort/) last year), but the heat was
too discouraging for her. Still, I decided to explore the trail we would have
taken together, and went down to the Naklice village. The trail towards it
is surprisingly flat, with occasional inclines that I could easily run over,
escaping from the numerous bugs and flies chasing me. Closer to the village
one has to cross Lisičina, a small mountainside stream flowing into Cetina
near Omiš. This time of the year, it was completely dry start to end.

![Sunset in the Naklice village](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-14.jpg)
![A mural in Naklice](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-15.jpg)

First thing I saw when I reached the village was a scenic bench overseeing
the sunset between the mountains. Although it was getting late and I wanted
to get home before sundown, I couldn't pass by such an opportunity, given I've
also seen a [scenic sunrise at Schneefernerkopf (2874 m)](/hikes/2026-07-19-schneefernerkopf/)
not so long ago. Rehydrating and taking in the view, I was thinking about my
route back to the city. All markings said that it takes about 90 minutes to
get there, although my gut feeling was telling me I can do it way faster.

![Lit up Omiš as seen from the trail near Naklice](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-16.jpg)
![A forgotten climbing route near Naklice](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-17.jpg)

I continued my way through the village and down the hills with no further
breaks, trying to beat the official time with as much margin as possible.
I passed Crkva sv. Ante and the local cemetery and took the main trail down.
As the sun was setting, the mountains got covered with the remaining pink
rays gently touching them. The evening breeze was also a welcome change,
although too late to make any lasting difference to my hike.

![Another cliffside bunker on the way to Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-18.jpg)

The snaky road down was also very well-kept. Paved with stones, it was easy
to walk on and had just the right incline to step on without any hurdle. As I
rushed down past the benches near scenic viewpoints, I noticed quite some
iconography written on the cliffs, pointing at long-forgotten climbing routes
and shortcuts. It really made me wonder if Omiš is less popular nowadays than
it used to be, at least for active sports. Maybe it's the people that got
lazier?

![Evening view of Omiš near the Cetina river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_celine/2026-08-08-19.jpg)

The remaining section of the hike was just as easy, and soon after I reached
the town, bustling with night life and covered in fanfare lights. For people
with some hiking experience behind them, I'd definitely recommend the route I
took. Take some long clothes with you, more water, and you'll be fine. For all
other readers, take the official lighter route. It's surprisingly nice,
especially compared to the other one. Have fun!
