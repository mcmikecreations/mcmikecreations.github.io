The idea of the hike came up when I was exploring the area around Tegernsee,
realizing that there were many interesting ridges I've never considered
visiting before. One of them was Hirschberg (1668 m), the highest peak on
its ridge. Since I didn't want to go there alone and none of my usual hiking
buddies were available, I decided to organize a DAAD Freundeskreis outing, and
sent out the invites.

![Setting of for the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-00.jpg)
![Crossing a bridge on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-01.jpg)

It was already quite windy and cold, and the hike itself wasn't the
easiest, but quite a lot of people showed up. Some were very sporty and chatted
the whole time, while others were more quiet and focused on the hike. The
beginning of the route goes through a decent forest trail, having occasional
steps or wooden bridges over the tiny creeks.

![The first alpine meadow on the route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-02.jpg)
![Progressive messages on a sign](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-03.jpg)

After a while, we reached the meadows, where the views opened up and we could
see the surrounding mountains. At that point, the less prepared members of the
group started to struggle a bit, especially mentally, since they realized that
the hike was more challenging than they expected. However, we kept going and
eventually reached the bottom of the mountain.

![The view at the bottom of the mountain](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-04.jpg)
![Rauheck-Alm (1485 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-05.jpg)

Although we didn't see that many hikers besides us on the way,
Hirschberg (1668 m) seems to be a popular destination, with many huts along the
way. We walked past some of them, taking a more direct route to the top. The
mountain is also generous in its trails, with many different paths leading to
the summit from all directions.

![View from the slope towards Tegernsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-06.jpg)
![View towards Tegernsee from the hilltop](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-07.jpg)

After walking for a while, we started seeing snow and realized that the way
ahead would feature harsher conditions. Thus, we took a break and asked a
passing hiker to take some group photos. One of the DAAD FK members was using
an e-cigarette the whole time on top of not being in the best condition for
hiking, so I was a bit worried about them reaching the top. However, they were
determined to make it, and we continued together.

![View towards Blauberge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-08.jpg)
![View at Hirschberg Vorgipfel (1653 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-09.jpg)

The last part of the ascent is walking on the ridge between Hirschberg (1668 m)
and Hirschberg Vorgipfel (1653 m). While the rest of the group was more tired
and just wanted to reach the main peak, I took two energetic members of the
group to the Vorgipfel, which had a better view of Tegernsee.

![Walking on the ridge towards Hirschberg (1668 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-18.jpg)
![Our group having alone time at Hirschberg Vorgipfel (1653 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-19.jpg)

We posed for some photos there, and I took the opportunity to learn the names
and locations of the surrounding peaks, which I hadn't memorized before.

![View from Hirschberg Vorgipfel (1653 m) towards Buchstein (947 m) and Hochplatte (1592 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-10.jpg)
![View from Hirschberg (1668 m) towards Auerkamp (1607 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-11.jpg)

After taking some photos and enjoying the view, we headed back along the ridge
to the rest of the group, and managed to catch up to them near the cross. Once
we reached it, took our photos and briefly talked to a few local hikers, my
plan was to start heading back down. The buses were scheduled to leave at
really inconvenient intervals, so I wanted to make sure we had enough time to
get back to the bus stop. However, the majority of the group wanted to stay on
the summit for a while, so we ended up spending more time there that I
expected.

![Our group at the top of Hirschberg (1668 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-12.jpg)
![View from Kratzer (1545 m) towards Hirschberg (1668 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-13.jpg)

Once every member was ready and eager to head back, we started descending, this
time choosing a different route that went through Kratzer (1545 m). I didn't
check the map very carefully and didn't realize that the route would include a
small slippery via ferrata section, but luckily, it was short and nobody had
any issues with it. The rest of the descent was uneventful, with us going down
on a narrow trail through the mountainside, and then through the forest to the
bus stop.

![Descending from Kratzer (1545 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-14.jpg)
![The via ferrata section at Kratzer (1545 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-15.jpg)

At the end, we had 40 minutes to spare before the bus arrived, so the group
split up, with some people going along the road to Rottach-Egern, while others
stayed and waited for the bus.

![A cow on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-16.jpg)
![Some grass on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hirschberg/2024-11-17-17.jpg)

All in all, this is a great hike with a lot of variation in the terrain and
difficulty. You can easily choose the route that suits you best, and the views
of the lakes from the top are stunning. Have fun!
