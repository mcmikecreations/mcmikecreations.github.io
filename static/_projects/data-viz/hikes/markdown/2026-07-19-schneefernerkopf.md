Our chair organizes yearly "scientific retreats" (where they isolate us and
force us to be productive). This year it was happening at Schneefernerhaus,
a research station on the slope of [Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/),
the highest peak of Germany.

![View from Ehrwald to Sonnenspitze (2417 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-00.jpg)
![View from Ehrwald to Schneefernerkopf (2874 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-01.jpg)

After visiting [Drachensee](/hikes/2026-06-21-drachensee/) and [Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/hikes/2026-07-12-drachenkopf/),
I was feeling like the Ehrwald area had much more to offer. Since there is a
very well known route to Zugspitze (2962 m) over Gatterl (2024 m), I decided
to take it instead of using the cable car with everyone else. Soon after I
looked at the map and saw a via ferrata towards Schneefernerkopf (2874 m) not
far from my destination, so the plan changed. I've never seen the sunrise on a
high mountain, so I took this obligatory trip as a chance to fulfill a
long-standing dream of mine.

![The 360° sunrise climb of Schneefernerkopf (2874 m)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5yRsnelCsw)

I set off from Ehrwald at 20:30, arriving by a surprisingly punctual train. At
first, I kept meeting other hikers on the road, returning from their trips.
Some even asked me about my route, being very surprised that I planned to hike
overnight. My body wasn't prepared to start hiking so late. The first few
kilometers were quite rough, with me pushing through despite getting out of
breath every few minutes.

![View towards Ehrwald from the nearby hills](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-02.jpg)
![View towards Ehrwald from Ganghofer Blitz](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-03.jpg)

As I kept going, the sky turned dark and the nature went to sleep. I still met
a few hikers with flashlights running down from their hikes in the area, but by
the time I reached Ehrwalder Almbahn Bergstation, I was the only one there
apart from the cows.

![View towards Ehrwalder Almbahn Bergstation](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-04.jpg)
![View from Issentalköpfl (1925 m) towards Ehrwald](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-05.jpg)

After I passed the station and looked at all the cows grazing around despite
the late hour, I planned to go to Gatterl (2024 m). However, I found the
Issentalköpfl (1925 m) summit not far from my route, and my inner completionist
screamed at me to go there too. To improve my peak bagging for the [Hiking Web](/hikes/tag/Web/),
I climbed a random steep ski slope there, stepping over small streams and mud,
and reached the plateau. The trail there was awful. Overgrown with spruce
trees and bushes, I had to climb through them, scratching myself all over.

![View at cows near Issentalköpfl (1925 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-06.jpg)

As I reached the supposed peak and found no cross in the dark, I turned around
and jumped from surprise seeing a bunch of cows around. Knowing how many hikers
die yearly from them, I made sure to be careful and walk as far away from them
as possible.

![Road from Issentalköpfl (1925 m) towards Am Brand (2116 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-07.jpg)

Afterward, I continued towards Am Brand (2116 m). By this point the clouds
have descended onto the slopes and my visibility dropped to a few meters in
front, even on the most powerful setting of the headlamp.

![View from Am Brand (2116 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-08.jpg)

The saddle of Am Brand (2116 m) was uneventful, apart from me avoiding yet more
cows along the way. I kept going and eventually reached Feldernjöchl (2045 m),
my last chance to turn around and walk back. I was feeling largely fine, so I
decided to continue the hike.

![Via ferrata near Feldernjöchl (2045 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-09.jpg)

Soon after, I saw a pair of dog-like eyes staring at me from 10 meters away. It
was a wild fox on the hunt. After staring at each other for some time, the fox
finally decided to leave and rushed uphill perpendicular to the road I was
using.

![The gate at Gatterl (2024 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-10.jpg)

After the last saddle, the road quickly turned into a via ferrata. It was quite
an easy one despite the darkness, and it was easily doable with no equipment
(that I was too lazy to pull out of my backpack). After some more walking, I
reached the gate at Gatterl (2024 m), after passing which I have officially
walked onto the Zugspitzplatt.

![The place of worship at Ehemalige Gatterlhütte](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-11.jpg)

The trail from here was very easy, without significant elevation changes. I was
getting quite hungry and didn't have any sense of distance, so after reaching a
small place of worship on the way I decided to take a break at a bench for some
snacks.

![Approaching Knorrhütte (2052 m) from Gatterl (2024 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-12.jpg)
![Night sky near Knorrhütte (2052 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-13.jpg)

I kept walking and walking, fighting my eyes closing and me falling asleep as I
stood, and eventually I saw a light in the distance. Since there was nothing
else around, it was obviously Knorrhütte (2052 m). I kept going there and some
30 minutes later reached the hut. The trail connects to it slightly above the
entrance, and I was too lazy to go down, so I just took a breather and kept
hiking. This point happened around 03:00.

![Climb uphill after Knorrhütte (2052 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-14.jpg)
![First peek at the Zugspitze (2962 m) summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-15.jpg)

The section between Knorrhütte (2052 m) and Sonnalpin (2588 m) is one of the
longest during this hike. It took me about 2 hours to hike between these points
since I didn't see the poorly marked route and had to occasionally climb the
slippery moist gravel.
I kept walking and at some point started seeing the light of the cable car
station at the top of Zugspitze (2962 m).

![First peek at Sonnalpin and Zugspitzplatt (2588 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-16.jpg)
![View from Sonnalpin (2588 m) towards Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-17.jpg)

Another 30 minutes and I also started seeing Sonnalpin (2588 m), although at
that point only through the night mode on my camera. After some more wrong
turns and climbing I reached the plateau cable car station and took a break.
There, I saw Schneefernerhaus, my final destination for the day. I knew that
now was the only chance for me to reach any peaks, since I won't be able to
step out of work later otherwise.

![View at Sonnalpin (2588 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-18.jpg)
![View at Reintal from Sonnalpin (2588 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-19.jpg)

I took one last look at the Reintal valley that I [hiked in the past](/hikes/2025-06-21-zugspitze_reintal/).
Despite the beauty of the sunrise and the clouds flowing through the plains,
I was facing away from them, starting my climb of Schneefernerkopf (2874 m).

![View into Austria from Schneefernerscharte (2699 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-20.jpg)
![View towards Reintal from Schneefernerscharte (2699 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-21.jpg)

The climb at 05:00 started weird, I couldn't find the right way up onto the
ridge. On Sunday, before heading out, I checked a few YouTube videos of people
climbing the peak, so I had some idea of where to go. Still, being there
in-person was tougher. Using my shoe spikes to climb over the ice and snow of
the last few remaining glaciers, I reached Schneefernerscharte (2699 m) and
started my way up.

![View towards Sonnenspitze (2417 m) from Schneefernerkopf (2874 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-22.jpg)

After another 30 minutes of climbing, I finally reached the fake summit. There
was a cross at 2836 m pointing towards Austria, making it visible from below.
It was also the entrance point to the "New World" ski tour route that descends
from Zugspitze (2962 m) down to Ehrwald. After taking some photos, I continued
uphill on the unsecured ridge trail and reached the summit at roughly 05:40.

![The real peak of Schneefernerkopf (2874 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-23.jpg)
![Descent from Schneefernerkopf (2874 m) towards Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-24.jpg)

The route back was uneventful. Although climbing up I felt scared for the way
down, in reality it was very doable, especially if you clip to the provided
cables. When I reached the saddle and walked onto the glacier again, I was
relieved that the hike was coming to an end. Still, I was overly happy about
this trip, it being one of the best experiences I've had so far. I then reached
Sonnalpin, rested for a few hours, then met the others and took the cable car
to Schneefernerhaus.

![Working at Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-25.jpg)

Although I wouldn't recommend doing the whole hike at night like I did, the
route from Ehrwald is by far the easiest route to the top of
Zugspitze (2962 m). If you take a few liters of water, some warm clothes (the
temparature drops to 2 degrees Celsius at some points), and some snacks, this
route is achievable for people of quite varying skill levels. I have to warn
you about the via ferrata section near the top though. Do **NOT** attempt it
without equipment!

P.S. I decided to include some photos from Schneefernerhaus too below.

![One of radioactive rooms at Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-26.jpg)
![The main passenger cog train tunnel of Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-27.jpg)

Although I was practically sleeping for the next two days, I did manage to
sneak out and do some exploring at night on Monday. We got to see most of the
station, including the passenger and cargo cog train tunnels, radioactive
laboratories, seismic sensors and more.

![Emergency exit and cargo cog train tunnel of Schneefernerhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-28.jpg)
![The peak of Zugspitze (2962 m) we visited on the way back](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-29.jpg)

We even got to visit the rooftops of the building and see the Milky Way one
more time without any light pollution. It was definitely a trip worth
remembering.

![The cable car from Zugspitze (2962 m) towards Eibsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/schneefernerkopf/2026-07-19-30.jpg)

On the way back we visited the peak and travelled down to [Eibsee](/404/),
where we then took the train home.
