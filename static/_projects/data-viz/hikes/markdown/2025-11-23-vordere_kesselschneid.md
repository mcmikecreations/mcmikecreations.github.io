With the previous spontaneous [hike to Pyramidenspitze (1997 m)](/hikes/2025-02-02-pyramidenspitze/)
still fresh in my mind, I was eager to reach the next mountain peak of
Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m) in the Kaisergebirge range and cross the 2 km
boundary, since I did not manage to do so last time due to time constraints.

![Festung Kufstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-00.jpg)

This time, I made sure to plan ahead and avoid any pitfalls from the previous
attempt. I bought gaiters to protect my lower legs from the snow and ice, and I
also packed less food and more hot water to keep me warm during the hike.
I took some basic medicine and magnesium supplements to help with muscle
cramps.

![Zottenhof](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-06.jpg)
![Gasthaus Veitenhof (724 m) and onwards](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-01.jpg)

For the hike itself, I selected a more direct route to the final summit,
skipping Petersköpfl (1745 m) and Elferkogel (1916 m) to save time and energy.
Instead of looking for a bigger group to hike with, I decided to go with Bohdan
from my recent [hike to Krottenkopf (2086 m)](/hikes/2025-11-16-krottenkopf_bischof/).

![Icicles on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-05.jpg)

We took the second earliest train from Munich to Kufstein, started the hike
around 7:30. The first section to Ritzaualm (1161 m) went by pretty quickly. On
the last attempt, the clouds were covering the valley, so we stopped many times
to take pictures and have group breaks. This time, the weather was clear, so we
limited ourselves to a few quick photo stops and went straight up.

![View from Vorderkaiserfeldenhütte (1388 m) onto Naunspitze (1633 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-07.jpg)

After passing Vorderkaiserfeldenhütte (1388 m) we started the real ascent among
snow and frozen spruce trees. The trail was mostly packed down, so we could
walk without shoe spikes, but it still slowed me down compared to normal
hiking. We made good progress until the crossroads to Petersköpfl (1745 m),
where the packed trail ended. From there, we had to trudge through fresh snow
all the way to the last summit.

![Road to Einserkogel (1924 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-08.jpg)

The next section to Einserkogel (1924 m) was quite annoying in my mind. The
trail (or lack thereof) goes through a "forest" of small spruce trees and
bushes. While the route is kind of visible because of blank spots among the
trees, you still have to constantly dodge branches and bushes, which slows you
down significantly. On top of that, you can't see far in any direction, so the
section feels never-ending.

![Path between Einserkogel (1924 m) and Zwölferkogel (1912 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-02.jpg)
![View down from Zwölferkogel (1912 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-09.jpg)

After passing Zwölferkogel (1912 m) and descending down a small via ferrata, we
reached Vogelbad at around 12:00. At this point, I already knew we were close
to our goal and we can make it back to Kufstein before dark.

![View from Zwölferkogel (1912 m) onto Pyramidenspitze (1997 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-03.jpg)
![Via ferrata at Vogelbad](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-10.jpg)
![T4 way down from Vogelbad](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-04.jpg)

We pushed on to
the Pyramidenspitze (1997 m) peak. The route was quite straining because of the
deep snow and strong wind. Bohdan led the way, but his footsteps were
disappearing as soon as he made them, so I had to make up my own route.

![Towards Pyramidenspitze (1997 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-11.jpg)

Reaching the top by 12:15, we took a few minutes to take pictures and check out
the via ferrata route going down to Walchsee, but we didn't linger long because
of the cold wind. We started our descent towards
Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m).

![View from Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m) towards Pyramidenspitze (1997 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-13.jpg)

After passing the small saddle between the two peaks, we ascended the final
stretch to Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m), the summit without a cross. The wind
was even stronger here, so we took only a few pictures of the top at 12:45 and
looked at the route to Hintere Kesselschneid (1995 m) before starting our
descent. It looked quite sketchy, with a lot of exposed rock and ice, so we
decided to skip it for now. I'll keep it for the future, maybe combining it
with the via ferrata route from Walchsee.

![Me and Bohdan at Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-12.jpg)
![View from Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m) towards Großer Roßkaiser (1978 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-14.jpg)

Once we were ready with our photos, we started our descent back to
Öchselweldkar. The trail was steep and slippery, with a lot of stones and ice
hidden beneath the snow. We had to be very careful with each step to avoid
slipping, although we did manage to slip a few times anyway. We also saw
mountain goats and some fox tracks on the way down. At some point, I also
noticed another hiker reaching the summit of Pyramidenspitze (1997 m) alone by
following our tracks. He then turned around and went back the way he came,
without descending to the river valley.

![The steep descent from Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-15.jpg)
![Mountain goats masking in front of the bushes](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-16.jpg)

At an early point still close to the summit, I slipped off an edge of a cliff
and started falling down a steep drop. While falling, I got a leg stuck between
two rocks, but it didn't stop my fall. To avoid bending and breaking my leg, I
slammed my hands against the rocks, trying to catch myself and slow down. While
this plan worked, I still felt a lot of pain in my leg, so I stopped to massage
it a bit before continuing down. It hurt, but I knew it wasn't broken, just a
bruise and some scratches. After a few minutes of massaging, I started feeling
less pain in my leg, which prompted me to realize that my hands were also
hurting a lot from the impact. In fact, I couldn't move my right pinky finger
at all! There was nothing I could do about it at the moment, 11 km from
Kufstein, so I just put the hiking sticks in my left hand and continued down.

![Continuing the descent](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-17.jpg)
![View from the slope onto Öchselweidschneid (1814 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-18.jpg)

After finally going down to Kaiserquelle, the rest of the way back to
Ritzaualm (1161 m) was more or less flat and uneventful. This section of the
route is going through a forest, so the wind doesn't reach here, making it much
warmer. The whole way to the alm, which we reached at around 15:30, I was
complaining about how annoying the trail was, going up for no apparent reason.
Now that I think about it, it was probably my pain making me grumpy.

![Forest on the way back](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-19.jpg)

At the hut, I asked for some painkillers, got two tablets and kept going down.
Given it's my second time visiting the Ritzaualm (1161 m), I have only positive
things to say about it. The staff is friendly, the food is good, and the prices
are reasonable for an Austrian alpine hut. I highly recommend checking out the
[website](https://www.ritzaualm.com/) for the opening hours and visiting it.

![Ritzaualm (1161 m) in the afternoon](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-20.jpg)

Continuing the way down, I asked Bohdan for another stop at
Gasthaus Veitenhof (724 m) to get some mountain cheese, the same one I bought
last time. Despite the earlier hour and more visitors on the trail, the hut was
almost empty, with the staff cleaning up before closing. I didn't want to make
small talk because of the situation with my hand, so I just quickly bought the
cheese and left. Last time, however, I remember the employee telling me about
how american soldiers stationed nearby used to come to the hut for some rest
after their field exercises in the mountains.

![Road back to Kufstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-21.jpg)

The rest of the way back to Kufstein was uneventful. We had to hurry as usual
to reach the train on time, making it a few minutes before departure. On the
train, I finally took off my gloves to check my hand. The finger was swollen
and purple. I came home, cleaned the scratches and the smeared blood from my
leg, took a shower and fell asleep. The next day, I went to an Orthopedics
clinic to discover my finger is fractured and my ligament is also damaged.
I got a loose cast for the finger and convinced my family doctor to prescribe
me painkillers. They weren't necessary for this hike, but I'd prefer to have
them ready for future hikes, just in case.

![The streets of Kufstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/vordere_kesselschneid/2025-11-23-22.jpg)

Overall, this hiking route is great and offers quite some stunning views of the
surrounding peaks, the taller Ellmauer Halt (2342 m) peak in particular. The
view towards Walchsee and in the direction of Salzburg is also quite nice.
On a sunny day, you can even see Wendelstein (1838 m) in the Bavarian Alps.
But I would heavily discourage doing this hike in winter, especially without
proper equipment and experience. The snow and ice make the trail much more
difficult and dangerous, especially on the descent. We crossed several warning
signs and closed trail signs, which were there for a reason. And most
importantly, make sure to hike with a partner who can help you in case of an
emergency.

P.S. In hindsight, I was lucky to not break my finger completely or hurt other
limbs. Still, in terms of physical conditioning, I felt quite good during the
hike, with no signs of fatigue or cramps compared to my previous attempt. The
cheap tall gaiters from Decathlon did their job well, keeping my lower legs dry
and warm.
