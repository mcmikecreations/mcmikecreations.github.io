title: 'Bockerlbahn-Weg: I am the Train'
description: Following a 1919 railway path near Spitzingsee.
ascent: 280

---
Every time I [walked near the Josefsthaler Wasserfälle](/404/),
I was intrigued by the wide shallow road going downhill. After some online
exploration, it turned out that the road was [part of a narrow railway](https://www.gamssteig.de/touren/bockerlbahnweg-spitzingsee)
taking wood from the nearby forests to Neuhaus. The whole route is a well-known
hike, so I wanted to experience it for myself.

![Starting the hike at Spitzingsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-00.jpg)
![The carved rocks near the Bleckstein hut](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-01.jpg)

When I got to the end of the trail near Waitzinger Alm and turned around, I was
immediately greeted with the railway road carved through rocks and hills to
avoid sharp turns and steep slopes. On some sections, since the weighted wagons
only travel in one direction, the rails were laid onto steeper slopes. I passed
the Bleckstein hut and saw a bunch of hikers resting there, but didn't visit it
myself.

![View towards Albert-Link-Hütte (1053 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-02.jpg)
![View towards Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-03.jpg)

I kept walking and getting closer to Spitzingsee, passing by a bunch of elderly
hikers. At one point I saw Jägerkamp (1746 m) that [I've been at in the past](/hikes/2024-08-31-aiplspitz/).
It was a memorable experience, just as the [trip to Rotwand (1884 m)](/hikes/2024-09-29-rotwand/).

![The meadow near Spitzingsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-04.jpg)
![Spitzingsee beach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-05.jpg)

When I approached Spitzingsee, walking on the meadow that operates as a ski
route in winter, I wanted to end the hike there. However, the trail keeps going
all the way to Neuhaus, so I had to see it to the end.

![Spitzingsee from the hillside](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-06.jpg)
![Brecherspitz (1685 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-07.jpg)

When I passed Spitzingsee and moved higher up the hill, I walked past a bunch
of cows and a few hikers taking the trail. The grass was much taller on this
section, making me believe most people skipt it and walk down to
Josefstaler Wasserfälle. I wasn't paying much attention to where I was stepping
and was focused on Brecherspitz (1685 m) I [want to visit](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/).
When I noticed a grandma in my peripheral vision, I looked down and saw a cat
she had on a leash! That goofy animal was not listening to her at all,
exploring the road at its own pace. I guess if you have all the time in the
world as a grandma, you can get the most bizarre hobbies.

![A train carriage monument on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-08.jpg)

I walked over some restored and a couple ruined bridges and passed a train
carriage monument. I read that the bridges were record-breaking at the time,
but since they were constructed from wood, they rotted and fell apart
eventually.

![Mountainside view form Neuhaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/bockerlbahnweg/2024-10-03-09.jpg)

When I was approaching Neuhaus, I checked the train schedule and
realized I was late as usual. To sum up, I'd say this is a great family hike
and a decent road for beginners. The elevation change is small and not steep at
all by design, so it's accessible for strollers and big families.
