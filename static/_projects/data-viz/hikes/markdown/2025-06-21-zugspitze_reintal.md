After much consideration, I and David decided to choose the Reintal route to
hike up Zugspitze, the highest mountain in Germany at 2962 m. We chose the
earliest train and bus connection to Garmisch Olympia-Skistadion, which
marks the start of the route.

The day before, we negotiated how much food to bring, and I ended up carrying
the following items for both of us:

![Food for the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-01.jpg)

As a little spoiler to not mention it later, the food and liquids we brought
were way too much. It was more difficult to carry it all and I had to take
toilet breaks at every other hut, which was quite annoying and slowed us down.
Out of the variety of food we brought, the Frühstücksfleisch (canned breakfast
meat) was the best, if eaten with bread or crackers, I highly recommend it. We
ended up not needing or using the energy drink, and I still have it unopened
as of August.

In the morning, David brought extra food and drinks to eat on the way, since
the travel time to Garmisch was quite long. I woke up at 3:45 AM, got onto S-Bahn
to München Hauptbahnhof at 4:41 AM. Surprisingly, I got my Deutschlandticket
checked on S-Bahn at 5:04 AM. We started hiking at 7:30 AM, making breaks
roughly every hour.

![View of Partnachklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-02.jpg)

First, we came to Partnachklamm shortly before 8:00 AM and waited for it to
open. There was a crowd of around 10 hikers waiting, who walked along with us
most of the way to the top.

We continued along the route, passing by some slower hikers and eventually
reached Bockhütte. At this point, mountain bikers began to pass us quite often,
making us stay alert and step aside frequently.

![View from Bockhütte to Kleinwanner (2548 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-03.jpg)

At this point the weather became quite hot, so we put on sunscreen and took
another break. Just as the previous sections, the following part between
Bockhütte (1052 m) and Reintalangerhütte (1366 m) is mostly flat, with a few light uphill sections.

After Reintalangerhütte (1366 m) there's a small bridge with a SUP tied to it, that
apparently hangs there the whole year, because I've seen tons of photos with
it on Google Maps and other websites. We reached the bridge at around 11:20 AM.

![The bridge near Reintalangerhütte (1366 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-04.jpg)

Shortly after the bridge, the route becomes significantly steeper, and the
actual climb towards Knorrhütte (2052 m) begins. Most people started taking breaks
more often on this section, so everyone got together and started ascending
in one big group.

![The view down from "Felsensteig zur Knorrhütte" signpost](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-05.jpg)

![Forget-me-nots (blue), Germander Speedwell (purple), and Saxifraga (white) on the way to Knorrhütte (2052 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-06.jpg)

We kept going, and reached Knorrhütte (2052 m) at around 1:00 PM. At the hut, we wanted
to order a drink and eat one of our snacks, but the waiter prohibited us from
eating our own food (dick move). Thus, we quickly finished the drinks and left the hut to
eat in front of it, outside the premises.

![Terrain near Knorrhütte (2052 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-07.jpg)

From the hut, you can clearly see the Gatterl (2024 m) mountain pass, which is
part of the Ehrwald route to Zugspitze (2962 m). It's slightly shorter, but more annoying
to get to if you live in München.

![A look at Gatterl (2024 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-08.jpg)

As we continued our way to the Zugspitzplatt station, the road up slowly
deteriorated, revealing more and more rocks, gravel, and snow. At no point did I
feel the need for crampons or winter shoes, but it is possible to get your feet
wet or stuck in the snow. We reached the station at around 3:40 PM.

![A look at the station on top of Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-09.jpg)

The next section from the Zugspitzplatt station (2943.75 m) (huh?) to Zugspitze (2962 m) summit is the most
difficult by far. The road is steep and completely covered in gravel and rocks
for half of the way, and the other half is a moderately steep via ferrata,
although it is very nicely secured and well-maintained.

![Gravel path to the summit station](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-10.jpg)

![The via ferrata section to the top](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-11.png)

The gravel section really took all of my strength, as for every two steps you
take upwards, you slide back one step down. The via ferrata section is not that
difficult, but since it comes directly after the gravel section, and it's already
late, it can be quite exhausting to push through to the station.

We reached the summit at around 4:50 PM, and were faced with a choice: either take
the last cable car down from the station or go to the cross at the peak and walk
down Reintal. We chose the latter, since we wanted at least some proof of us
being at the top. We took a few minutes to rest and went to the door leading to
the last via ferrata to the peak. Turns out, the door was locked, although
there were a few people still enjoying the view at the top. I went to one of the
employees and asked if we could go through, which he denied. I asked what the
people at the top were supposed to do, and he said that it's not his problem.

![The peak and cross of Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-14.jpg)

Thankfully, the door and passage at the station open from the other side, so the
people at the top came down and held the door for us. We climbed up and reached
the cross at around 5:30 PM.

![The cables securing via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-12.jpg)

![View from the top down to Eibsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-13.jpg)

Although we were constantly on the lookout for the employees scaring us off, they
never did. We took a bunch of photos, but the clouds were quite low, so the view
was not as good as it could have been.

![The photo model at the top (Mykola)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-15.jpg)

![The photographer at the top (David)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-16.jpg)

We ate some Käsespätzle at the station Bistro, which was still open due to the
celebration of [Bergfeuer zur Sommersonnenwende](https://www.meinbezirk.at/reutte/c-lokales/das-staunen-beginnt-mit-sonnenuntergang_a7398380)
(lighting a fire on the day of longest
sunlight). At that point there was no way down besides walking, so we started
the descent.

![Evening at Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-17.jpg)

The descent was quite long, and at around 8:40 PM, still on the way to Knorrhütte (2052 m),
we saw a rescue helicopter flying around, trying to pick up a mountain climber who
fell down from Jubiläumsgrat (appr. 2740 m) and caught him-/herself on a ledge. The helicopter
had to let the rescuer down on a rope uphill from the climber, who then climbed
down, secured the victim, and brought him/her up to the helicopter. In the meantime,
while the rescuer was climbing down, the helicopter had to circle around and
survey the area, flying really low to the ground, giving us a great view of the
event. After the victim was rescued, the helicopter made another round to pick
up the rescuer, and flew away. We were quite lucky to witness this event, where
everyone was safe and sound in the end.

![Mykola shocked by the helicopter rescue](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-18.jpg)

We got down to Knorrhütte (2052 m) at around 10:00 PM, mostly because we spent 2 hours
looking at the helicopter rescue. We took a short toilet break, and continued.

![Back at Knorrhütte (2052 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-19.jpg)

As we put on our headlamps, the night came, and we continued our way down in
the dark, surrounded by flies and mosquitoes. We had to switch to the red LED
of the headlamps to avoid attracting them, so everything around us turned a
dark red, while the sky became deep blue and cyan. Since there was no light
pollution, we could see the whole Milky Way, which was a great sight.

![Back down to Reintalangerhütte (1366 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-20.jpg)

We also came across fireflies (less than ten million), frogs/toads,
mountain mice, a mountain goat, and tons of different insects. The way down
took longer than expected because of the darkness, and we reached Reintalangerhütte (1366 m)
at around 0:15 AM.

![Our last brain cells forcing us to record something near Reintalangerhütte (1366 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-21.png)
![Bike parking near Reintalangerhütte(1366 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-22.jpg)

The rest of the way was surprisingly challenging. We were already tired, so we felt
every rock and piece of gravel under our feet. Because of the darkness, we couldn't
tell our position and speed, and not wanting to disappoint ourselves, we kept going
blindly, thinking that we're almost there. The temperature also quickly dropped,
especially along the Partnach stream, with wind bringing cold moist air from the
stream up to us. We tried taking a break to eat, but the cold made us immediately
continue. We also met some runners wearing headlamps, who were running up to
Reintalangerhütte (1366 m) and back down, which was quite impressive.

![Detour around Partnachklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-23.jpg)

We kept going slower and slower, and at around 4:00 AM we reached Partnachklamm.
The gorge was closed, so we had to take a detour around it, slowing us down even more.
On the detour, David's feet started hurting even more, and I slipped on wet wood
on some steps and fell down, so we slowed down to a crawl.
We kept going, walking past new hikers who were just starting their way up,
and finally reached the bus stop at the ski slope at around 6:30 AM. We were
completely exhausted, so we fell asleep on the train back to München.

![Mykola sleeping on the train](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-24.png)

All in all, this was an amazing experience, although the road back from Knorrhütte (2052 m)
felt like filler content nightmare. While the route up is great and not too difficult,
the way down to Knorrhütte (2052 m) with the evening views is great, the rest of the way
to Garmisch is just pure suffering, not worth the effort.

![Compilation of the road to Zugspitze (2962 m) over Reintal by [David Bilan](https://bilandavid.com/)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/zugspitze_reintal/2025-06-21-00.mp4)

I wouldn't repeat this route the way we did it the first time. With better
resource management it's completely possible to reach the top before the last
cable car leaves, which I'd do if some other group suggests going up Reintal.
Another option would be to go down a different route, e.g. the via ferrata to
Eibsee. It's also possible to do a big section of the route with a bike rented
in Garmisch, that cuts down on hiking time by about 2-3 hours in each direction.
Lastly, the huts along the way usually have a few overnight places
left and cost only 30 Euro per person, which is a great option if you're
pressed for time and don't want to walk overnight.
